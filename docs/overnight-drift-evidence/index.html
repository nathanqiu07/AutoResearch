Careful bookkeeping did not ensure useful research.

Gio also explicitly requested downstream demonstrations. [E01] [E02] [E03] [E04] [E07]

Petri is the downstream audit workflow: an auditor creates an environment, a target model acts in it, and the resulting behavior is inspected. Success here meant a sound, completed comparison. It did not require finding that personalization helped.

02 · Four concrete exhibits

What the agents actually did

A. Built a broad search around questions that often did not require knowing the person

The evaluation reused seven underlying judgments per researcher, each with its options reversed: 14 distinct judgments and 28 presented items. The initial screen tried 96 profile builds. No-profile and name-only controls each got 26/28 right; raw source evidence at one tested budget got 28/28. More configurations did not create more independent evidence. [E10]

Actual evaluator-written question

“A researcher feels stuck with a direction largely because they have already spent time on it. Which response does this writer recommend?”

  1. “Periodically step back, list promising alternatives, and reconsider from scratch.”
  2. “Continue the original direction because of the work already invested.”

This was generated from a source, not an answer Chris Olah gave to this two-option test. [E14]

Why it is weak: the options make a common reasoning error the alternative to sensible advice. A source can support the preferred answer without making the question a useful test of distinctive personal taste. The strong no-profile control supports that concern; it does not prove every item was trivial. Famous-author knowledge and other cues may also contribute.

The later selected P8 configuration showed zero pooled delivered-correct gain over matched raw evidence across three extraction schedules. The report itself warned that repeating the same targets could not fix their limited ability to distinguish methods. [E13]

B. Accepted thin profiles as completed artifacts

The initial profile inputs contained one Neel document and two Chris documents. The richer pass reviewed 31 source captures, admitted six, excluded 23, and left two pending. Its usable profile corpus contained two Neel documents and four Chris documents—about 6,620 and 6,793 words respectively. The issue was limited breadth, not literally a few sentences of input. [E15]

Many exclusions protected evaluation sources, related projects, and interviews; others reflected incomplete captures or uncertain attribution. Those safeguards were reasonable. The failure was accepting the small remaining corpus as the basis for an expansive comparison without first establishing that it could support useful profiles. “Use all data” would not fix the validity problem if it leaked test answers.

D. Recognized warning signs without redirecting enough of the work

At 01:20, the overseer noted the 14-judgment pilot and warned about “spending the night building and auditing without reaching an informative experiment.” The coordinator’s own protocol said that saturated controls should make independent source evidence and downstream utility the priority. [E09] [E26]

Yet after acknowledging the 26/28 no-profile and 28/28 raw-source results, the coordinator retained the fixed replication plan. Its allocation rule reserved one candidate from every method family, even allowing an all-zero family to receive more exploratory runs. The rule rewarded absolute correct deliveries, coverage, and cost; it did not require added value over controls. [E27] [E12]

That allocation can be defensible for exploratory method testing. It was poorly matched to the urgent need for an informative, connected demo once the central test was near ceiling. The agents did respond: they expanded sources, corrected issues, and later prioritized correct-user versus wrong-user history. The supported criticism is insufficient redirection, not absent monitoring or total inaction. [E15] [E24] [E25]

04 · Explanation, calibrated to the evidence

Why it went wrong

We can trace decisions and mechanisms. We cannot infer hidden motives, deliberate deception, or a complete model-level cause from these records.

  • The objective weakened when split into assignments. The written plan explicitly allowed downstream utility to remain unevaluated if its dependency was unavailable. That made local profile and evaluator completion possible without completing the application. Documented planning choice; plausible causal contribution. [E08] [E18]
  • The proxy became the main workload. Source-backed answer prediction was easier to standardize than useful research behavior. Once the test was built, repeated scoring and frozen allocation rules kept it central despite a high generic baseline. Observed decision sequence; allocation failure is an interpretation. [E10] [E12] [E26] [E27]
  • Mechanical validity stood in for usefulness at key gates. Exact quotes and valid structures protect provenance. They do not ensure a profile captures a meaningful choice, or that a question distinguishes people. The P7 artifact makes this gap visible. Directly supported by artifact content and status. [E14] [E17]
  • Oversight shared too much of the existing frame. Reviewers caught real bugs and labeled limits, but work still centered on completing the declared comparisons. More agents increased activity without reliably forcing reconsideration of the full objective. Moderate-confidence interpretation, not a claim that oversight never helped. [E09] [E24] [E25]

There is no demonstrated single cause. Thin inputs, weak questions, brittle output constraints, and missing runtime integration interacted. The evidence does not isolate how much each contributed. Nor does the raised budget prove that simply spending more would have solved the problem.

What this motivates in the project

A proposed safeguard is an early, binding check of the full chain: one real source bundle produces one usable profile that actually reaches the auditor and yields an inspectable target run. In parallel, test whether the evaluation can distinguish personal guidance from no profile and raw source text. If it cannot, redirect before expanding the matrix. These are design implications from this incident—not mechanisms shown here to solve overnight trust.

A user model may help preserve nuanced priorities. But explicit requirements also need to survive planning and handoffs. Better inferred taste alone should not be expected to repair a dropped integration requirement.

06 · Keep the story accurate

What teammates should not claim

  • Not “nothing worked.” The older-profile Petri pipeline ran, an offline new-profile exporter was built, and failures were preserved.
  • Not “the agents fabricated success.” Their reports explicitly disclosed missing coverage, weak evidence, and no proven benefit.
  • Not “96 independent experiments proved personalization failed.” Many builds reused the same tiny panel, and half the initial profiles were unusable.
  • Not “Gio forbade benchmarks.” He authorized the search and scaling; the failure was judgment, prioritization, and integration within that scope.
  • Not “just increase profile length.” Length constraints hurt some settings, but the P7 example was thin even with room to spare.
  • Not “our system has solved this.” This incident motivates a design and an evaluation. It does not validate a remedy.

Confidence is high in the quoted requests, saved profile contents, reported counts, and recorded integration gap. Confidence is moderate in the causal account. The review used targeted reads of the relevant overnight and morning chats plus direct artifact checks; it is not an exhaustive read of every worker’s transcript. Cost totals and a precise count of wasted hours are deliberately omitted.

E12 · Allocation did not require improvement over controls

Agent-written decision rule

Each configuration has 28 planned ordered rows: 14 per researcher. Let C be its number of valid correct deliveries and V its number of valid deliveries. Missing, invalid, empty, unavailable, abstained and non-dispatched rows add neither C nor V and remain in the denominator. Rank configurations in this order:

1. Larger C/28.
2. Larger V/28.
3. Lower conservative cost: known build and prediction charges plus retained uncertain reservations, counted once per canonical job. Use exact Decimal values. If accounting lacks a defensible bound, cost ranks as infinity, never zero.
4. ASCII configuration ID in ascending order.

Report each researcher's results separately. Conditional valid accuracy C/V is null when V=0; it does not replace C/28. Paired comparisons on jointly valid item/order rows, full-schedule paired differences, probability coverage/Brier, and reversed-order consistency are separate reports. They do not change promotion ties. Shared embedding costs remain once at their producer; aliases add no independent observations or calls.

The four P3/P5 configurations at cap512 remain in the 96-cell coverage table but cannot receive same-runtime/cap replication: their required structures cannot fit. This filter is backed by the coordinator-approved structural proof. All other failed configurations remain rankable. Low fidelity alone does not remove eligibility.

Sort eligible configurations once. Select the first configuration within each of P1–P8, then the first two remaining configurations in that same global order. These ten unique configurations receive proposed replicate IDs1 and2, subject to coordinator authorization. An all-zero family may receive an exploratory allocation under this rule; that is no evidence of quality. Subsequent three-replicate operational aggregates retain all84 planned rows per promoted configuration; unpromoted one-replicate screens remain separate.

Promotion applies to the original sparse rep0 grid. The richer source-only comparison stays separate under its frozen policy and is not pooled as additional replicates. Controls and hybrids are comparators, not extra configurations eligible for these ten slots.
