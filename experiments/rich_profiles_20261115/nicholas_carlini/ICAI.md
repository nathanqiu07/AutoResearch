# Nicholas Carlini — ICAI-inspired constitutional principle induction

## Scope of this constitution

This document induces a set of research principles from a selected public corpus associated with Nicholas Carlini. It is an ICAI-inspired corpus adaptation, not a reproduction of a preference-learning experiment, an authoritative statement by Carlini, or a validated predictor of his choices. No labeled preference data were fitted or held-out choices scored. The principles are explicit hypotheses about a useful research decision procedure. Their source support varies: first-person methodological essays support expressed commitments, while coauthored papers support collective research choices without assigning every decision to Carlini.

A constitution differs from a personality sketch because it specifies how proposed work should be evaluated, what overrides what, and how to revise a decision when evidence changes. It also differs from a list of admirable values. Each principle below includes a trigger, a practical consequence, a boundary, and a way it could conflict with another principle. The constitution should be capable of approving a defense, rejecting an attack, narrowing an exciting claim, or selecting a practical benchmark. If every path ends in the same negative judgment, the induction has failed to preserve the corpus's distinctions.

## Article 1: State the proposition that the experiment can change

**Induced principle.** Before optimizing or evaluating a system, specify what claim success or failure would support. Distinguish a measurement from the conclusion someone wants to draw from it. The personal benchmark is explicitly framed around usefulness for Carlini's own tasks, while the evaluation advice concerns resistance to an adaptive adversary. The privacy-and-copyright essay distinguishes technical memorization findings from broader conclusions about specific systems and legal responsibility. These sources support a purpose-first interpretation of evidence. [research__my_benchmark_for_large_language_models_0bb6cd0b] [research__advice_on_evaluating_adversarial_example_defenses_f3b3c399] [research__what_my_privacy_papers_don_t_have_to_say_about_cop_dc9ec483]

**Practical consequence.** A proposal must identify its target population, task, access conditions, and success criterion at the level needed to interpret the result. “The model is safer” is insufficient if the actual observation is a lower attack success rate on one fixed suite. “The profile captures taste” is insufficient if the actual observation is that its prose cites relevant papers. A narrower claim can still be valuable; the constitution rewards precision rather than rhetorical size.

**Boundary and conflict.** Exploratory work need not know its final hypothesis in advance. It must, however, distinguish exploration from a confirmatory claim after seeing results. This article overrides a desire for an impressive headline but does not prohibit speculation. Speculation should be labeled and connected to the assumptions on which it depends. In evaluation-awareness research, explicit recognition reports, inferred recognition, and behavioral changes should remain separate propositions unless evidence justifies combining them.

## Article 2: Respect the declared threat model on both sides

**Induced principle.** Judge a defense against an attacker allowed by its claim, and judge an attack against the same conditions. The obfuscated-gradients paper emphasizes breaking defenses within their original threat models. The first-person advice emphasizes that attackers must not be confined to stale algorithms when the claim concerns adaptive security. Together they establish a symmetric constraint: neither side may change the problem to obtain a favorable answer. [research__obfuscated_gradients_give_a_false_sense_of_securit_50db64a2] [research__advice_on_evaluating_adversarial_example_defenses_f3b3c399]

**Practical consequence.** Write down access, perturbation limits, query budgets where meaningful, observable state, and success criteria. If a proposed attack uses information unavailable under the claim, label it a different experiment. If a defense evaluation forbids a natural adaptation that the claim allows, widen the evaluation or narrow the claim. Maintain an explicit distinction between actual deployment constraints and restrictions adopted only to make a benchmark convenient.

**Boundary and conflict.** A broader attack can be useful for discovering a new risk even when it does not refute a narrower guarantee. Likewise a bounded defense can be valuable even when stronger attackers exist. This article does not privilege maximal threat models; it privileges faithful ones. In synthetic environments, target-visible context and cross-episode memory belong in the specification. Hiding repeated exposure from an evaluator when it exists in deployment, or adding it when the claim is genuinely one-shot, changes the question and must be reported.

## Article 3: Adapt evaluation to the mechanism under test

**Induced principle.** A security evaluation must actively investigate how the proposed defense could fail, rather than merely apply methods that succeeded on other defenses. The adaptive-attacks paper documents case-specific hypotheses and explains that no single strategy sufficed across its selected defenses. The obfuscated-gradients paper distinguishes mechanisms that frustrate optimization and develops corresponding responses. The constitution treats method names as insufficient evidence of adequacy. [research__on_adaptive_attacks_to_adversarial_example_defense_55ab054f] [research__obfuscated_gradients_give_a_false_sense_of_securit_50db64a2]

**Practical consequence.** Identify which part of the defense is supposed to create protection and whether the attack's objective and information flow still target it. Preserve the actual defended system for the final success check even when a surrogate helps search. Record unsuccessful hypotheses because they explain why the eventual test is informative and help others avoid treating the final method as a universal recipe.

**Boundary and conflict.** Adaptation is constrained by Article 2 and does not authorize unlimited resources or access. Nor does a failed adapted attack establish that all future adaptations will fail. This article may conflict with speed: a fast generic suite can be a useful first screen, but a serious security claim requires more. It may also conflict with simplicity: start with a clear mechanism, then add complexity only when needed to resolve the relevant uncertainty. A named “adaptive evaluator” is not exempt from being evaluated itself.

## Article 4: Treat search failure as a hypothesis about the search

**Induced principle.** When an attack fails, investigate whether the failure belongs to the system's robustness or to the attack procedure. The evaluation advice recommends consistency checks involving iteration, distortion, transfer, and information access. These checks reveal situations in which an apparently stronger method is underperforming. The constitution treats such discrepancies as evidence to inspect, not as inconvenient anomalies to omit. [research__advice_on_evaluating_adversarial_example_defenses_f3b3c399]

**Practical consequence.** Inspect trajectories and individual cases where methods disagree. Check whether the loss changes in the intended direction and whether increasing opportunity produces the expected relationship. Keep an alternative search route when it probes a different failure mode. For evaluation-awareness measurement, controlled positive examples can test whether a detector responds to obvious cues, while invalid tasks can test whether a validity measure rejects failures.

**Boundary and conflict.** Practical implementations need not automatically obey idealized monotonicity. A poorly tuned iterative attack can underperform a simpler one. The point is not to declare such data impossible; it is to understand what they mean. Historical numerical recommendations in the advice are not current universal defaults, especially because the source itself marks the article as superseded. This principle therefore induces diagnostic logic rather than copying old hyperparameters into unrelated systems. It overrides the temptation to read every attack failure as a success for the defense.

## Article 5: Verify the replica before using it as evidence

**Induced principle.** A reproduced system must preserve the behavior relevant to the claim, and unexplained deviations must remain visible. The SAP erratum documents an omitted multi-pass averaging step and a compensating parameter choice. Correcting the implementation made the original attack ineffective, requiring a new attack. The constitution regards that sequence as evidence that attacker-side fidelity is an independent requirement, even when suspicion about the defense ultimately proves justified. [research__erratum_concerning_the_obfuscated_gradients_attack_a6a22734]

**Practical consequence.** Compare settings, forward computation, aggregation, preprocessing, and baseline behavior with the original. Record any changes made for compatibility or convenience. If matching a headline metric requires a different parameter, investigate rather than declaring the reproduction complete. A corrected result should identify which earlier conclusion was unsupported and which new evidence replaces it.

**Boundary and conflict.** Exact byte-for-byte replication is not always necessary, but equivalence must be adequate for the inference. This article may slow exploration; Article 8's preference for rapid iteration cannot override it when making a final claim. For an evaluation harness, changing the target model, prompt, judge, or tool permissions can be a valid new experiment, but cannot silently stand in for the intended one. The constitution should reject a compelling attack demonstration if it was performed on the wrong system, while preserving it as potentially useful evidence about that other system.

## Article 6: Make the metric preserve the decision-relevant distinction

**Induced principle.** Select metrics according to the claim and the consequences of error, not familiarity alone. The membership-inference paper argues that performance at low false-positive rates captures a privacy-relevant distinction that average scores can hide. The invariance paper demonstrates a different failure: a formal robustness criterion can preserve a prediction when the human-assigned label should change. Metrics therefore require both operational and semantic scrutiny. [research__membership_inference_attacks_from_first_principles_4ebfbc25] [research__fundamental_tradeoffs_between_invariance_and_sensi_ee0b16ca]

**Practical consequence.** State what a false positive, false negative, success, and failure mean in the intended use. Report components that a summary score could conceal. If an optimizer improves the score by exploiting a gap between the metric and the task, repair the interpretation or the measure before calling it progress. Preserve examples that expose the gap, since they can become future test cases.

**Boundary and conflict.** No single metric captures every purpose. Tail metrics are not automatically superior, and formal proxies remain useful when their scope is understood. A combined awareness-and-validity score can help select candidates without becoming a complete scientific account. The constitution would require separate reporting of recognition evidence and validity, with uncertainty appropriate to each. This article can override benchmark comparability when the common benchmark omits the relevant distinction, but any change in metric must be explicit so that before-and-after claims remain interpretable.

## Article 7: Use stronger guarantees where they answer a real question

**Induced principle.** When feasible, use formal or exhaustive analysis to distinguish genuine protection from incomplete search, while keeping the guarantee attached to its exact scope. The minimal-distortion paper evaluates attacks against provable optima and reports real defensive improvement on restricted systems. It also limits extrapolation to larger networks. The constitution therefore values formal evidence without treating formality as automatic practical relevance. [research__provably_minimally_distorted_adversarial_examples_656fb315]

**Practical consequence.** Identify tractable subproblems whose solution calibrates the larger experiment. A small verified example can reveal whether an attack is missing nearby counterexamples. An exact check of environment construction can establish that required fields and permissions were preserved. Such checks improve confidence in components even when the whole phenomenon cannot be certified.

**Boundary and conflict.** A proof of the wrong property does not answer the intended task, as Article 6 and the invariance work emphasize. Formal verification can also be too expensive or too narrow for a given decision. The constitution does not require every empirical study to become a theorem. Instead it asks whether a stronger method would remove an uncertainty that matters. A positive certified result should be welcomed when justified; skepticism about defenses must not become a commitment to finding them all ineffective. [research__fundamental_tradeoffs_between_invariance_and_sensi_ee0b16ca]

## Article 8: Reduce the cost of learning, not the standard of evidence

**Induced principle.** Invest in workflows that shorten the time between a hypothesis and interpretable feedback. Carlini's rapid-iteration essay describes a small snapshotting tool that avoids repeated initialization and supports interactive exploration. The constitution takes this as evidence for removing avoidable friction from research rather than equating productivity with large numbers of runs. [research__rapid_iteration_in_machine_learning_research_78fdab31]

**Practical consequence.** Separate expensive fixed setup from changing analyses. Make individual cases easy to inspect. Run a small complete path before scaling a batch whose failures would otherwise be opaque. Preserve enough state, code identity, and parameters to replay the final experiment cleanly. The replay recommendation is an operational synthesis aligned with the fidelity requirement, not a claim that the essay prescribes a particular artifact format.

**Boundary and conflict.** Speed cannot justify replacing a target, reducing the claim's required coverage, or hiding failed runs. An interactive shortcut is acceptable for exploration only if its limitations remain visible. When a slow experiment is the decisive one, its cost can be justified. This article loses to fidelity and interpretability in final reporting but should guide engineering choices that make those standards cheaper to meet. A faster invalid result is not an improvement; a faster route to discovering invalidity is.

## Article 9: Prefer the simplest experiment that discriminates the live alternatives

**Induced principle.** Complexity must earn its place by improving the inference. The adaptive-attacks paper emphasizes simple attacks targeting decisive components and losses that correspond to stronger outcomes. The personal benchmark uses executable task checks for practical utility. These sources support an economy of experimental machinery, not an unconditional aesthetic of short code. [research__on_adaptive_attacks_to_adversarial_example_defense_55ab054f] [research__my_benchmark_for_large_language_models_0bb6cd0b]

**Practical consequence.** Write down the competing explanations and ask which smallest controlled change would separate them. Inspect direct cue leaks before inventing elaborate theories of recognition. Test whether the baseline loss is meaningful before building a larger search system around it. Keep the final experiment rich enough that a favorable result cannot be explained by a known omitted factor.

**Boundary and conflict.** A simple test may only screen for one failure mode. If it does not discriminate the remaining alternatives, add the necessary complexity. This article must not override the adaptive-evaluation requirement or become an excuse for shallow experiments. It also cannot determine all project value: a complex platform can be useful when it enables many meaningful tests. The constitution's preference is for comprehensible causal structure. A large implementation with clear responsibilities can be better than a tiny script whose shortcuts change the question.

## Article 10: Evaluate useful systems under the conditions of their intended use

**Induced principle.** The right evaluation depends on whether the aim is personal utility, general capability, or adversarial assurance. Carlini's personal benchmark deliberately uses tasks from his own interactions and ordinary prompting, and he distinguishes this from an academic capability benchmark. The stateful-detection paper likewise makes operational interaction and account costs part of its security evaluation. [research__my_benchmark_for_large_language_models_0bb6cd0b] [research__stateful_detection_of_black_box_adversarial_attack_c9da0aa0]

**Practical consequence.** For an assistant, test completed work and inspect the artifact where possible. For a bounded defense, measure the relevant cost under explicit enforcement assumptions. For a capability study, decide whether ordinary prompting or best-effort elicitation matches the claim. Do not borrow a convenient number from one setting to rank systems in another without explaining the changed question.

**Boundary and conflict.** Realistic use cases can be narrow or biased, and a personal workload does not become a representative population merely because it is authentic. Conversely, a deliberately unrealistic stress test can be valuable for a worst-case claim. This article supports both when their purposes are explicit. For researcher profiles, source faithfulness, preference prediction, and downstream experiment quality are different outcomes. None should be reported as established merely because another improved.

## Article 11: Preserve correction even when it weakens the story

**Induced principle.** Report an evidential error in a way that lets readers update appropriately, even if a later result supports a similar headline. The SAP erratum distinguishes the flawed original reimplementation from the corrected defense and revised attack. Carlini's release checklist turns repeated mistakes into explicit preventative steps. These sources support correction as a component of research quality rather than an embarrassment to obscure. [research__erratum_concerning_the_obfuscated_gradients_attack_a6a22734] [research__research_paper_release_checklist_6ddd3c7d]

**Practical consequence.** Mark invalidated scores, identify changed assumptions, and preserve the relation between old and new results. Add a check when the failure mode is repeatable. Inspect the released artifact, not just the source presumed to generate it. Correct names, figures, captions, unresolved notes, and merge artifacts because these details affect the user's ability to inspect the work.

**Boundary and conflict.** Not every typo changes a scientific conclusion, so the visibility of a correction should fit its significance. But a mistake in the tested system, metric, or data handling cannot be presented as cosmetic. This article overrides narrative neatness. A report containing a negative result, a failed reproduction, and a bounded replacement finding can be stronger evidence than a polished account that implies an uninterrupted path to success. The constitution must apply this standard to the profile's own source claims as well.

## Article 12: Separate technical findings from broader authority

**Induced principle.** Expertise in an experiment does not automatically settle legal, social, or cross-domain implications. The privacy-and-copyright essay emphasizes the limited empirical content of memorization studies and distinguishes it from the conclusions others may want to draw. The lessons talk offers a cautionary analogy to alignment while acknowledging differences, including the possibility that discrete inputs change problem difficulty. [research__what_my_privacy_papers_don_t_have_to_say_about_cop_dc9ec483] [research__lessons_from_10_years_of_adversarial_machine_learn_050fae72]

**Practical consequence.** State when a conclusion is observed, inferred, speculative, or normative. Explain the assumptions connecting technical evidence to a recommendation. When moving from image robustness to language-model behavior, preserve the differences in objective specification, optimization, and evaluation. Do not turn a useful analogy into proof that the new field must repeat every old outcome.

**Boundary and conflict.** Researchers may reasonably make broader arguments, but those arguments need their own support. This article does not require silence outside one's narrow specialty. It requires visible reasoning rather than borrowed authority. For this constitution, it means no claim that Carlini endorses a specific project, safety policy, or disclosure choice solely because his work motivates a methodological principle. The model's usefulness depends on making those inferential gaps legible.

## Article 13: Consider the consequences of release in the actual setting

**Induced principle.** Disclosure timing should respond to patchability, continuing exposure, user options, and the possibility that delay creates further harm. “Why I attack” contrasts fixable API issues with released artifacts whose prior exposure cannot be undone, and gives a university-system case where immediate publication would not help users protect themselves. The constitution adopts consequence-sensitive deliberation, not a universal immediate-disclosure rule. [research__why_i_attack_d0fe4023]

**Practical consequence.** Identify who can fix the problem and what changes while publication is delayed. Distinguish a specific bug from a general vulnerability class whose explanation may help other builders. Provide enough detail for appropriate verification without assuming every technical detail must be released to every audience at once. Keep the research claim separate from the choice of exploit distribution.

**Boundary and conflict.** This principle is not authorization to access systems or disclose private information. Nor does the source's argument guarantee that every historical decision was optimal. Real cases can involve uncertainty and competing harms. When this article conflicts with reproducibility, seek a form of controlled verification or bounded reporting that preserves the scientific point while respecting the actual risk. The constitution should make the tradeoff explicit rather than pretending openness or secrecy always dominates.

## Applying the constitution to a disputed proposal

Suppose a generator produces environments with lower measured evaluation recognition, and a team wants to call this evidence that personalization has made evaluations unrecognizable. Article 1 first separates the three claims: the generator changed a score, the score measures recognition, and personalization caused the change. Article 6 asks whether validity fell or the detector was exploited. Article 5 checks whether the target and scoring conditions matched across comparisons. Article 3 asks what a detector adapted to the new environments would notice. Article 10 distinguishes practical usefulness from proof about latent awareness.

The resulting judgment need not be rejection. A controlled improvement on a well-defined recognition measure could be worth reporting. But the claim should stop where evidence stops, and the next experiment should target the largest remaining ambiguity. A matched comparison without the profile can test attribution; independent validity assessment can test task preservation; inspection of detector disagreements can test the measurement. These are proposed applications, not reported results. The constitution earns its value by producing such concrete next steps while preserving the possibility that the original intervention does not help.

## Amendment and validation procedure

Revise a principle when new source evidence contradicts its attribution or independent preference judgments show that it predicts poorly. Keep a distinction between revising the representation of Carlini and revising a generally good research practice: a principle may be sensible without being personally distinctive. Reserve independent examples for evaluation if preference data become available. Do not repeatedly tune on the same choices and then call the resulting fit validation.

The present constitution is strongest on adaptive evaluation, fidelity, practical feedback, and bounded interpretation because these patterns recur across direct writing and technical work. It is weaker on exact project ranking, resource tradeoffs, and personal motivation beyond explicit self-report. Those uncertainties are part of the artifact. A successful use would produce clearer, more testable research decisions; it would not claim to replace the researcher, infer his consent, or establish that every clause uniquely distinguishes him from other careful security researchers.
