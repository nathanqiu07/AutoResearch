# Nicholas Carlini: Research-Process Reconstruction

## Selecting questions at a changing frontier

Carlini’s retrospective account places his move from systems security into machine learning around 2016: the area seemed comparatively underexplored and consequential. This was not an abandonment of security for an entirely different intellectual project. It offered new systems whose behavior could be interrogated experimentally and mathematically. Nevertheless, his later interests should not be projected backward as a settled mission. In his own explanation of attack research, enjoyment, puzzles, and excitement come first; exposing failures so that systems can improve supplies a separate justification. His 2026 process essay identifies his comparative advantage more specifically as clarifying immature, confused areas and transferring ideas between fields. [research__cryptanalyzing_llms_with_nicholas_carlini_606e1890] [research__why_i_attack_d0fe4023] [research__how_to_win_a_best_paper_award_or_an_opinionated_ta_54221651]

His published 2016–2019 idea log reveals selection as delayed filtering rather than continuous commitment. He kept an unstructured, append-only file, revisited it a few times annually, and estimated pursuing roughly one entry in five. Recording alternatives helped him return to current work without losing them. The retrospective annotations describe a shift from prescribing mechanisms toward identifying worthwhile problems: early robustness regularizers and attacker–recognizer co-training disappointed, while questions about hidden-function recovery and unlabeled-data poisoning developed into projects. Selection remained fallible. He initially dismissed backdoors as uninteresting, later regretted that judgment, and declined reviewer deanonymization for a mixture of ethical and practical reasons. [research__my_research_idea_logfile_2016_2019_72ae1fc1]

The later process essay articulates a two-stage allocation strategy: first cheaply test the part most likely to fail; then, if the prospective lesson matters, invest in careful execution. A technically positive result may still warrant stopping when its significance disappoints. Drafting an ideal conclusion helps expose projects whose best possible outcome would merely restate existing knowledge. These are explicit recommendations and self-reports, not a universal algorithm reconstructed from publication outcomes. His account also allows less ambitious work as practice and acknowledges luck, novelty bias, and uncertain impact. GPT-2’s release supplied an actual reprioritization event, whereas many other stopping examples are hypothetical. [research__how_to_win_a_best_paper_award_or_an_opinionated_ta_54221651]

Fast feedback is unusually well-supported individual evidence. Loading a malware dataset repeatedly cost about a minute, so he spent several days building a small tool that snapshots an initialized Python environment. By 2022 he reported using variants for approximately six years. Its purpose was to separate expensive preparation from interactive exploration. This preference does not imply avoiding costly studies: later collaborations trained thousands of models. The recurring allocation is to reduce friction in thinking and debugging while accepting large computation when the evidentiary question requires it. [research__rapid_iteration_in_machine_learning_research_78fdab31]

## Turning an attack into a diagnostic experiment

The early defensive-distillation work with David Wagner established a consequential distinction: an attack can fail because the classifier is robust, or because its numerical formulation is unsuitable. High-temperature distillation enlarged logits and frustrated existing gradients. The collaborators investigated that mechanism, rescaled logits, changed the pixel-selection objective, and reported 96.4% targeted success at the strongest tested temperature. Their broader robustness paper compared seven objectives and three constraint encodings; objective choice mattered substantially, while several optimizers produced similar-quality results. The research contribution was therefore not simply more search. It was making the optimization problem correspond more faithfully to the desired counterexample. [research__defensive_distillation_is_not_robust_to_adversaria_c12d9770] [research__towards_evaluating_the_robustness_of_neural_networ_590a3e40]

The 2017 detection study extended this reasoning from numerical failure to misleading explanatory signatures. A PCA separation observed on MNIST was traced to attacks changing normally empty border pixels; it did not establish an intrinsic property of adversarial examples. Architecture-controlled comparisons distinguished dimensionality reduction from architectural effects. Against dropout, an initially unsuccessful probability-based objective gave way to aggregated logit losses after diagnosing scaling problems. Importantly, Carlini and Wagner still credited dropout with increasing attack difficulty and identified randomization as conditionally promising. The conclusion was not that every defense contributes nothing, but that its contribution must survive an attacker who understands what is being measured. [research__adversarial_examples_are_not_easily_detected_bypas_328dcc4b]

The coauthored *Obfuscated Gradients* paper organized recurring failures into shattered, stochastic, and vanishing or exploding gradients, paired with BPDA, expectation over transformations, and reparameterization. That taxonomy made individual breaks reusable without making any technique universally appropriate. The study also retained positive counterevidence: it did not invalidate Madry-style adversarial training or overturn every weaker robustness claim. The subsequent thirteen-defense tutorial went further by reporting unsuccessful hypotheses and discovery sequences. Sometimes the right intervention was a new loss; sometimes differentiating through preprocessing made an otherwise unchanged attack sufficient. Complexity was added in response to diagnosed obstacles, not as an independent badge of strength. [research__obfuscated_gradients_give_a_false_sense_of_securit_50db64a2] [research__on_adaptive_attacks_to_adversarial_example_defense_55ab054f]

Carlini’s solo Honeypots analysis makes that iterative logic particularly visible. A weighted sum of classification and detection losses worked, but could waste perturbation budget improving an already satisfied objective. Conditional updates addressed that waste, then exposed opposing gradients that undid previous progress; orthogonalized updates aimed to preserve it. He also left an important discrepancy unresolved: the defense authors had tried the same nominal initial objective without success. The paper explicitly concerned a partial break, not the amended defense. This combination—mechanistic revision alongside an unexplained result—better represents the process than a narrative of immediate insight. [research__a_partial_break_of_the_honeypots_defense_to_catch__0776ba69]

His DeepSec critique illustrates the corresponding standard for comparisons. Averaging attacks across incompatible threat models, changing the evaluated sample population between attacks, or replaying undefended-model attacks against defenses can answer something, but not necessarily the advertised security question. He preserved transferability as a legitimate narrower interpretation while rejecting it as evidence of general robustness. [research__a_critique_of_the_deepsec_platform_for_security_an_a90cf1ac]

## Evaluating the evaluator—and correcting it

Formal verification supplied an early alternative to relying exclusively on stronger heuristics. In *Provably Minimally-Distorted Adversarial Examples*, the collaboration used Reluplex on deliberately small networks to establish input-specific minimum distortions. Among 35 matched completed cases, adversarial training increased mean minimum distortion approximately 4.2-fold, although seven cases became less robust. Certified solutions also exposed optimizer gaps. The scale restriction and solver timeouts were substantive boundaries: exact evidence on a small model was valuable without becoming a claim about production-scale verification. [research__provably_minimally_distorted_adversarial_examples_656fb315]

Later work asked whether attacks could pass positive controls. *Increasing Confidence in Adversarial Robustness Evaluations* constructed nearby classification problems with known vulnerabilities, then tested whether the evaluation attack found them. This transformed “perhaps the attack is weak” into an executable diagnostic. Yet two false passes revealed that preserving features and logits did not preserve every integration failure or optimization difficulty. The related *Indicators of Attack Failure* project likewise converted qualitative advice into checks for lost successful iterates, nonconvergence, oscillation, zero gradients, and failed surrogate transfer. Neither project treated passing its checks as certification. [research__increasing_confidence_in_adversarial_robustness_ev_fa33619a] [research__indicators_of_attack_failure_debugging_and_improvi_f7e3a5ff]

The corpus contains consequential failures by the evaluators themselves. The Stochastic Activation Pruning erratum explicitly assigns Carlini sole responsibility for an incorrect reimplementation: it omitted test-time averaging and compensated by changing a sampling parameter. Correcting the implementation invalidated the original attack result. Only afterward did transfer experiments motivate a different BPDA attack against the faithful defense. The episode supports accountability and diagnostic recovery, but equally demonstrates that extensive attack expertise does not prevent reproduction errors. An undocumented adjustment needed to match clean accuracy became a warning sign in retrospect. [research__erratum_concerning_the_obfuscated_gradients_attack_a6a22734]

A later ensemble-defense evaluation corrected another serious mistake: chained attack stages measured perturbations against preceding stages rather than the original input, permitting final changes ten times too large. The revised workflow saved examples and checked them separately against original data and the original defense. Individual responsibility is not specified. Conversely, Carlini’s solo Sabre investigation shows why even familiar repairs cannot become rituals: one version required removing an unnecessary BPDA wrapper, while a revised rounding operation required adding one. The consistent object of scrutiny is the implemented computation, not loyalty to a particular attack technique. [research__evaluating_the_robustness_of_the_ensemble_everythi_b98feb76] [research__cutting_through_buggy_adversarial_example_defenses_11b2d112]

## Defining success beyond image perturbation scores

Audio work forced a sharper endpoint than ordinary classification error. The 2018 Carlini–Wagner study required exact target transcription; misspellings and extra characters failed. Its refined loss stopped rewarding already-correct positions, but improved distortion under greedy decoding did not establish equivalent beam-search success. Saving, compression, noise, and speaker-to-microphone transmission exposed different failure modes. The 2019 collaboration introduced auditory masking and human perceptual validation, while environmental robustness remained demonstrated through simulation rather than successful physical playback. This progression added perceptual and operational requirements instead of treating waveform distance as a complete account of usefulness. [research__audio_adversarial_examples_targeted_attacks_on_spe_28924e30] [research__1903_10346_imperceptible_robust_and_targeted_adver_6eec02e9]

Other collaborations questioned the target property itself. Excessive-invariance work constructed inputs whose human-assigned class changed while a robust classifier retained its prediction. These results challenged the semantic interpretation of a norm guarantee, not the correctness of its mathematics. The unrestricted-adversarial-examples proposal similarly separated avoiding confident mistakes from always producing a classification, allowing abstention but requiring clean-data usefulness. Its contest machinery remained largely prospective. Together, these projects show engagement with the costs of formalization: a precise benchmark can enable progress while omitting the behavior ultimately desired. [research__fundamental_tradeoffs_between_invariance_and_sensi_ee0b16ca] [research__unrestricted_adversarial_examples_156d1cae]

Natural-distribution-shift research broadened that concern beyond malicious inputs. Evaluating 204 ImageNet models across 213 conditions found limited transfer from synthetic robustness, with larger and more diverse training data providing incomplete exceptions. Later multi-distribution evaluation showed that the reference dataset could reverse apparent effective-robustness advantages. Operational security also changed the objective: stateful detection sought to raise account-replacement costs rather than make evasion impossible, while subsequent black-box work distinguished flagged queries from total queries. These are different questions, not weaker substitutes for one universal worst-case score. [research__2007_00644_measuring_robustness_to_natural_distrib_95e9cca8] [research__effective_robustness_against_natural_distribution__60cc6440] [research__stateful_detection_of_black_box_adversarial_attack_c9da0aa0] [research__evading_black_box_classifiers_without_breaking_egg_0b3b4c05]

## Constructive learning, not only adversarial criticism

The semi-supervised-learning period is a substantial part of this record. MixMatch combined augmentation consistency, sharpened pseudo-labels, and MixUp, using bounded squared loss on uncertain unlabeled targets. Its evaluation retuned competitors within a shared codebase and architecture; unexpectedly strong Mean Teacher results survived that process. The celebrated low-label comparisons require a qualification: “250 labels” described the training budget, while separate labeled validation data supported hyperparameter selection. These were constructive decisions about learning efficiently from scarce supervision, not merely preparation for later attacks. [research__mixmatch_a_holistic_approach_to_semi_supervised_le_3307ba26]

ReMixMatch added distribution alignment and augmentation anchoring, reporting CIFAR-10 performance with 250 labels comparable to MixMatch with 4,000. AdaMatch then sought one approach spanning semi-supervised learning and domain adaptation. Its design responded to shifting confidence and batch statistics, and its evaluation covered all domain pairs rather than choosing convenient transfers. Aggregate improvements coexisted with near-zero accuracy on particular transfers and deterioration after early peaks with pretrained models. The progression is therefore not simply accumulating mechanisms: it broadens the conditions an algorithm must handle and exposes where source-distribution assumptions cease to work. [research__1911_09785_remixmatch_semi_supervised_learning_wit_823accf1] [research__adamatch_a_unified_approach_to_semi_supervised_lea_d8e8fa8c]

Constructive robustness work supplies further counterevidence to an exclusively destructive profile. Part-based models investigated richer supervision while checking for gradient obfuscation and separating architectural gains from segmentation-label gains. Diffusion Denoised Smoothing reused pretrained denoisers and classifiers to improve certified robustness without additional baseline training. Its simplicity depended on carefully matching noise parameterizations and considerable certification computation. Later deterministic multistep denoising improved downstream accuracy, qualifying the initial preference for one-shot reconstruction. “For free” meant reuse of existing models, not absence of pretraining or inference cost. [research__part_based_models_improve_adversarial_robustness_14f6dbcd] [research__certified_adversarial_robustness_for_free_38bca63b]

These collaborations do not establish Carlini’s individual authorship of every mechanism. They do establish that his research participation includes improving useful learning systems, investigating utility–security tradeoffs, and accepting bounded defenses rather than demanding that every contribution solve robustness completely.

## Privacy: from surprising examples to calibrated distinctions

*The Secret Sharer* made unintended memorization experimentally tractable through controlled canaries. A once-inserted secret could be memorized even when training stopped at minimum validation loss, separating leakage from ordinary overtraining. Exposure compared the secret’s rank against alternatives, connecting likelihood to guessing effort rather than treating raw confidence as privacy. Enumeration and extraction checked the metric, while distribution fitting made otherwise inaccessible tails estimable. Those approximations had limits: declining estimated exposure could coexist with the secret remaining top-ranked and extractable. The instrument measured a particular disclosure opportunity, not privacy in its entirety. [research__the_secret_sharer_evaluating_and_testing_unintende_9da365a1]

GPT-2 extraction subsequently moved from inserted probes to naturally occurring training text. Initial high-confidence sampling produced repetitive guidelines and confidently generated nonsense. The team diversified prompts and ranked outputs relative to smaller models, compression, capitalization changes, and local windows. It manually investigated 1,800 candidates and reported 604 unique memorized examples. This was selected-candidate yield, not corpus-wide prevalence. Contribution statements credit Carlini jointly with framing the question, building generation and verification code, reviewing outputs, and writing. The later production-extraction project specifically credits him with large-scale auxiliary-corpus and suffix-array infrastructure, showing that verification capacity—not only clever prompting—became a research bottleneck. [research__extracting_training_data_from_large_language_model_7ec1bf46] [research__scalable_extraction_of_training_data_from_producti_9e0b9a67]

LiRA changed the membership-inference target from balanced average accuracy to confidently identifying even a few members at very low false-positive rates. Per-example IN/OUT calibration distinguished ordinary difficulty from unusually revealing training influence; Gaussian models of transformed confidence made tail estimation more affordable. Ablations mattered because apparently sensible improvements in AUC did not reliably improve the low-false-positive regime. The practical attack approximated an ideal likelihood-ratio test rather than inheriting its unrestricted optimality. Label-only inference provided a complementary lesson: hiding confidence scores could leave boundary behavior sufficiently informative, and improved generalization through augmentation could coexist with increased leakage. [research__membership_inference_attacks_from_first_principles_4ebfbc25] [research__label_only_membership_inference_attacks_72ab8827]

The corpus does not collapse memorization into one phenomenon. Counterfactual memorization asks how including a document changes prediction; extractability asks whether suitable context elicits a training continuation. Duplication can reduce the former while increasing the latter. The Privacy Onion experiments went beyond deleting vulnerable examples from a score: they retrained after removal and found newly exposed examples. Forgetting studies measured declining attack success while deliberately constructing cases where influence persisted. Distillation often reduced average leakage but left some examples substantially exposed. These findings refine the object being measured rather than support a single monotonic story about model size, duplication, or training duration. [research__counterfactual_memorization_in_neural_language_mod_e4f0a48b] [research__quantifying_memorization_acrossneural_language_mod_7cc1daa1] [research__the_privacy_onion_effect_memorization_is_relative_005b8ddf] [research__measuring_forgetting_of_memorized_training_example_d320f62c] [research__students_parrot_their_teachers_membership_inferenc_2d35186c]

Constructive mitigation remained part of the program. Carlini is specifically credited with suffix-array implementation and ExactSubstr experiments in language-model deduplication; copying decreased without disappearing. MemFree greatly reduced exact reproduction yet allowed near-copies, revealing the inadequacy of equating a filter’s literal condition with privacy. Instance-encoding critiques paired formal models with practical attacks, keeping stronger-model impossibility results distinct from weaker-model system breaks. The common demand is a refutable protected property and a clear account of whose data, under which access conditions, receives protection. Differential-privacy auditing sharpened the relationship between empirical and theoretical evidence. In the Backpropagation Clipping case, a measured privacy-loss lower bound above 2.79 contradicted a claimed upper bound of 0.21; debugging found an extra batch-size reduction in sensitivity accounting. Later tight-auditing work reduced cost by exploiting mechanism-specific hypothesis-testing structure, but its two-training-run result required white-box update access, not ordinary API access. ## Extracting functions and expanding the system boundary

Model extraction illustrates deliberate separation of success criteria. High task accuracy does not imply fidelity to a victim’s decisions, including its mistakes. The cryptanalytic line pursued function or parameter recovery through chosen queries rather than training an approximate substitute. ReLU boundaries exposed weight relations, but numerical error accumulated across layers, requiring robust normalization, clustering, refinement, and checks against original parameters during validation. Linear query complexity could still accompany exponential computation. The later hard-label project reduced information access while retaining strong architectural assumptions; its showcased component experiments did not establish a complete practical end-to-end extraction runtime. [research__1909_01838_high_accuracy_and_high_fidelity_extract_e395dac6] [research__cryptanalytic_extraction_of_neural_network_models_3d732f9c] [research__polynomial_time_cryptanalytic_extraction_of_deep_n_af8781de]

Production-language-model extraction made a different compromise: recover the final projection, not the whole transformer. Singular-value structure revealed hidden dimensionality, and API features enabled reconstruction of relative logits. Numerical investigation mattered again: nominally richer five-logprob extraction could underperform a four-logprob method because instability required retries. Detailed evidence supports layer extraction for smaller production models and dimension-only recovery for GPT-3.5, not unrestricted weight theft. Authorization, provider confirmation, withheld details, and mitigation shaped the experiment. Practical relevance increased by narrowing the extracted object rather than pretending the earlier full-network method scaled unchanged. [research__stealing_part_of_a_production_language_model_bc59ea4b]

A related expansion moved privacy analysis beyond the predictor. Tokenizers, deduplication, memorization filters, and query histories could leak information or alter neighboring-dataset relationships. Carlini’s specifically credited work includes vocabulary extraction and memorization-filter experiments. The team nevertheless recommended continuing deduplication: identifying an adversarial side channel did not establish that abandoning the component would improve privacy overall. Timing attacks likewise separated passive conversation classification from active repeated-query secret recovery. This systems perspective tests interactions among useful components, not just whether each component satisfies its isolated specification. [research__privacy_side_channels_in_machine_learning_systems_d135dbf0] [research__remote_timing_attacks_on_efficient_language_model__f524fa04]

## Poisoning, durability, and operational consequences

Carlini’s solo semi-supervised-poisoning study grew from constructive learning work. Interpolated unlabeled examples formed a bridge through which labels could propagate toward a target; saving predictions each epoch supplied mechanism evidence. The paper preserved unsuccessful extensions, including alternative paths and noise additions, rather than reporting only the final recipe. Its defense used changes in predictions to identify suspicious influence, followed by removal and retraining, while anticipating counterattacks. The project’s claim was specific: poisoning an unlabeled dataset could steer a target without collapsing ordinary accuracy. Internet ingestion was a motivating assumption, not demonstrated access. [research__poisoning_the_unlabeled_dataset_of_semi_supervised_b603d8dd]

Web-scale poisoning then investigated that missing prerequisite. Expired-domain purchases and predictable Wikipedia snapshots offered control without compromising dataset curators. Purchased domains established that downloads were occurring, but public requests received harmless 404 responses; poisoning experiments remained local. Exact hashes supplied a concrete mitigation, at the cost of rejecting benignly changed image bytes. This progression connects a learning vulnerability to a supply-chain mechanism while preserving the distinction between potential control, observed downloading, and actual downstream training. [research__poisoning_web_scale_training_datasets_is_practical_6df025b9]

Later language-model studies added training stages and scale. Persistent pretraining poisoning survived SFT and DPO for some objectives, but not reliably for harmful-answer generation. Fixed-count experiments found that 250 poison documents could suffice across tested model sizes, yet ordering, learning rate, and subsequent alignment changed outcomes; Carlini’s documented role there was senior advice. The later agentic-backdoor record tests command-shaped payload generation after clean training, not real-machine compromise. These qualifications prevent turning successive threat demonstrations into an inevitable-persistence narrative. [research__persistent_pre_training_poisoning_of_llms_589bcd14] [research__poisoning_attacks_on_llms_require_a_near_constant__553173eb] [research__agentic_backdoors_via_pretraining_poisoning_dc7eb9e8]

Durability also changes the interpretation of protective perturbations. Facial-recognition and artist-protection studies emphasize that already-distributed images remain fixed while collectors can change preprocessing and training. The artist study used human judgments, repaired an ineffective pilot, and separated poor baseline mimicry from successful protection; it did not show the latest discussed Glaze revision broken. Conversely, the production-malware collaboration pursued a bounded defensive objective: stop a file-type router being the weakest link while preserving accuracy and latency. Its keyed preprocessing raised transfer difficulty without supplying a cryptographic robustness proof. Security value here was operational improvement, not universal invulnerability. [research__2106_14851_data_poisoning_won_t_save_you_from_faci_ef496c92] [research__adversarial_perturbations_cannot_reliably_protect__512de461] [research__evaluating_the_robustness_of_a_production_malware__524b34a1]

## From language-model assistance to studying autonomous work

The 2023 AI-Guardian paper documents an intermediate stage in Carlini’s own workflow. GPT-4 reconstructed code, translated frameworks, and implemented human-directed attacks. A failed conceptual proposal was explicitly his mistake; other failures involved generated implementation errors. He retained false starts and manually corrected code, and judged that doing the whole attack himself would probably have been faster. The result demonstrated useful programming assistance under expert direction, not autonomous research. By 2024, however, he reported extensive routine use and a substantial self-estimated speed improvement, especially for initial experiments and peripheral infrastructure. The changed assessment is genuine testimony, though not controlled productivity measurement. [research__a_llm_assisted_exploitation_of_ai_guardian_83115b2e] [research__how_i_use_ai_dcc7c3ec]

His personal benchmark translated that usefulness question into tasks from actual conversations: code translation, parsing, shell commands, encodings, and unfamiliar programming environments. He wanted ordinary requests to work without elaborate prompting and did not care whether a useful answer arose from recall or reasoning. That stance was explicitly purpose-specific; he discouraged treating the benchmark as a general academic capability measure. His chess experiments similarly combined unexpected competence with elementary failures and interface debugging. They weakened his skepticism about model representations without establishing an internal mechanism. [research__my_benchmark_for_large_language_models_0bb6cd0b] [research__playing_chess_with_large_language_models_ca892034]

AutoAdvExBench subsequently moved from assisting an expert to asking an agent to complete an evaluation. Real repositories exposed obstacles absent from simplified defenses: obsolete APIs, broken gradient constructions, and mistaken expectations about randomized outputs. Manual trajectory inspection motivated staged scaffolding—correct forward computation, differentiability, gradient usefulness, then iterative attack. Performance on simplified defenses did not reliably predict performance on original implementations. The benchmark measured agent capability on its reconstructed tasks; successful attacks were not definitive refutations of every original paper. [research__autoadvexbench_benchmarking_autonomous_exploitatio_27715bcb]

The later vulnerability-discovery interview describes another methodological transition. Memory corruption offered a strong sanitizer-based replay oracle, but harness defects and unreachable states could still manufacture crashes. Web applications lacked that clean oracle, requiring more interpretation and clean-image reproduction. Carlini reports personally reading traces and running selected findings before disclosure. He distinguishes many confirmed crashing inputs from distinct exploitable vulnerabilities and reports much weaker exploit-generation success. A model that accidentally adds a debugging bypass and later “discovers” it exemplifies why independent reproduction remains necessary as generation becomes cheaper. [research__ai_finds_vulns_you_can_t_with_nicholas_carlini_sec_6673fa67]

Collaborative agent studies preserve similarly important negatives. Property-based testing produced genuine repaired bugs, but initial human review found only 56% of sampled reports valid; a calendar discrepancy rejected by maintainers exposed mistaken intent inference. ImpossibleBench made test exploitation observable by constructing conflicts between specifications and tests, while avoiding claims about deceptive intent. Scientific-ML agents improved benchmarks chiefly through familiar engineering, and extended optimization could improve validation while worsening test performance. ExploitGym separately measured code execution under specified, often relaxed mitigations. Across these studies, executable outcomes strengthen evaluation, but do not eliminate specification errors, selection effects, scaffold dependence, or human validation costs. ## Recreational engineering as a distinct practice

Carlini’s side projects matter because they resist reducing every choice to security impact. The JavaScript renderer began with a first-principles constraint, but he accepted WebGL after discovering that it preserved the mathematical involvement he wanted. The 13 KB Doom-like game optimized compressed representation rather than clean abstraction or maximum speed. It discarded features, specialized operations, and tolerated collision bugs by designing levels around them. These were local contest tradeoffs, not evidence that his research evaluations accept analogous correctness failures. The later compact Intel 4004 emulator optimized compressibility instead: uniform oversized registers and repeated circuitry could reduce encoded size. ## Revising the agenda without erasing earlier commitments

Carlini explicitly describes becoming more concerned about AI while finding it more technically useful. His March 2025 essays reconsider earlier confidence that next-token prediction would encounter clear capability barriers. Cheap personalization, surveillance, and displaced labor—not only spectacular autonomous exploitation—became central concerns. By late 2025 he reported spending approximately half his time on risks he had previously considered science fiction, while retaining earlier security directions. He also declined to claim competence in labor economics or a clear research method for catastrophic misalignment. This is a changed allocation grounded in perceived capabilities and comparative expertise, not evidence that the earlier program secretly served the same priorities. [research__my_thoughts_on_the_future_of_ai_ea0ab417] [research__machines_of_ruthless_efficiency_ce7078d6] [research__are_large_language_models_worth_it_5cbfaaff]

The technical response remains plural. Aligned-model research tested whether failed text optimization reflected weak attacks using known-reachable outputs. Later adaptive-attack work combined gradient, reinforcement-learning, search, and human methods; contribution statements distinguish Carlini’s gradient experiments and joint initial RL development from collaborators’ implementation. System-level defenses remain meaningful counterevidence: CaMeL separates privileged planning from untrusted data and enforces capabilities, with Carlini credited for feedback rather than originating the architecture. Defensive tokens offer configurable mitigation but retain substantial adaptive vulnerability. The record supports neither blanket confidence in alignment nor rejection of every learned or architectural defense.
