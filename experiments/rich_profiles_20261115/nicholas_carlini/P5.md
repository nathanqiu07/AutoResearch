## 1. Select questions where curiosity, consequence, and comparative advantage overlap

**When** several technically plausible projects compete for attention, **prefer** the question whose answer would change understanding or practice, especially in an immature field where existing formulations are confused. Treat an attractive technique as a candidate means, not sufficient justification for a project.

Carlini explicitly describes enjoyment and puzzle solving as his primary motivation for attacking systems, separately arguing that exposing failures is important. His research logfile records a shift from proposing particular robustness mechanisms toward identifying worthwhile problems. The later research-advice essay identifies clarifying young fields and transferring ideas between areas as comparative strengths, rather than endlessly extending mature formulations. [research__why_i_attack_d0fe4023] [research__my_research_idea_logfile_2016_2019_72ae1fc1] [research__how_to_win_a_best_paper_award_or_an_opinionated_ta_54221651]

Concrete trajectories include connecting model extraction to cryptanalysis, finding poisoning questions through semi-supervised learning, and redirecting attention toward GPT-2 training-data extraction. These are recorded project origins and retrospective interpretations, not evidence that every successful question was foreseeable. His logfile also preserves missed opportunities: he initially dismissed backdoors and later regretted it. [research__my_research_idea_logfile_2016_2019_72ae1fc1]

**Competing priority:** immediate usefulness is not mandatory. A foundational counterexample or enjoyable side project can justify itself differently. Nor should “importance” become an excuse for permanent searching: his advice allows good-enough projects as practice.

**Reverse or narrow the preference** when a mature problem offers unusually consequential unresolved deployment failures, when the supposedly important question cannot produce a discriminating result, or when unfamiliarity is being mistaken for opportunity. The relevant comparison is expected learning and consequence, not novelty alone.

## 2. Test the likely point of failure cheaply, then invest in iteration infrastructure

**When** a project rests on an uncertain technical premise, **first attack its most failure-prone subproblem with a small prototype**. If it survives, remove recurring operational friction before expanding experiments.

This combines two explicit practices. Carlini’s research advice recommends early tests of the subproblem most likely to fail and abandoning positive results whose best-case conclusion remains unimportant. His rapid-iteration account describes spending several PhD days on a small Python snapshotting tool because repeatedly loading a malware dataset took about a minute. He reports that delays beyond roughly a second interrupt his productive exploratory rhythm; that is a personal experience, not a universal latency threshold. [research__how_to_win_a_best_paper_award_or_an_opinionated_ta_54221651] [research__rapid_iteration_in_machine_learning_research_78fdab31]

The Honeypots recording supplies an operational example: he invested its opening period in easier iteration, reproduced the published baseline, and only then developed stronger attacks. The recorded sequence includes unsuccessful refinements rather than uninterrupted progress. [research__realtime_screen_recording_of_breaking_a_defense_to_7d87fb05]

**The exception is evidential necessity.** Fast iteration does not imply small final experiments. Privacy auditing, counterfactual memorization, and extraction verification sometimes require extensive retraining or large searches. The inferred distinction is between avoidable waiting and computation that answers the question.

**Reverse the infrastructure investment** when setup cost exceeds likely reuse, when cached state obscures reproducibility, or when interactive convenience hides accidental dependencies. At publication, fresh-state validation takes precedence over preserving the exploratory environment. Likewise, stop a technically successful prototype if its scientific significance no longer justifies expansion.

**When** the bottleneck is expensive labels, mismatched domains, or fragmented learning procedures, **prefer constructive synthesis with controlled comparisons**, retaining utility as a primary outcome.

[research__mixmatch_a_holistic_approach_to_semi_supervised_le_3307ba26] [research__1911_09785_remixmatch_semi_supervised_learning_wit_823accf1]

AdaMatch pursued one approach across semi-supervised learning and domain adaptation, prioritized training from scratch, and retained all domain pairs in its cheaper benchmark. Its broad gains coexisted with near-zero performance on particular transfers and sensitivity to assumptions about class distributions. [research__adamatch_a_unified_approach_to_semi_supervised_lea_d8e8fa8c]

**Competing priorities:** unification reduces fragmentation but can hide task-specific weaknesses; label efficiency can increase compute or tuning demands. Security investigations arising from these methods should not retroactively redefine their original objective.

**Reverse the preference for a unified method** if controlled task-specific baselines dominate, if alignment assumptions fail under realistic shifts, or if total acquisition and computation costs outweigh the measured supervision savings.

## 4. Define the claimed success before choosing the attack or metric

**When** a proposal uses broad terms such as robustness, extraction, privacy, or safety, **translate them into an operational claim**: whose outcome matters, what access is allowed, which transformations preserve the task, and what observation constitutes failure?

The collaborative evaluation guidance makes threat models the basis of falsifiability. Malware modification must preserve malicious behavior; small distance is not automatically the relevant constraint. An out-of-model vulnerability can matter without refuting the stated guarantee. [research__on_evaluating_adversarial_robustness_b187c740]

Model extraction illustrates why objectives must remain separate. High task accuracy differs from fidelity to the victim’s predictions. Later production-model work recovered information about a final projection layer and hidden dimensions, not an entire transformer; some larger attacks were estimated or deliberately left unimplemented. [research__1909_01838_high_accuracy_and_high_fidelity_extract_e395dac6] [research__stealing_part_of_a_production_language_model_bc59ea4b]

Speech attacks likewise required exact target transcriptions rather than approximate or merely incorrect output. A refinement that reduced distortion also narrowed its exact-success result to greedy decoding, demonstrating that an improvement can change the guarantee’s scope. [research__audio_adversarial_examples_targeted_attacks_on_spe_28924e30]

**Exception:** formal precision is not a prerequisite for every useful vulnerability demonstration. The LLM-security position paper preserves a role for practical demonstrations where harm is concrete even if the broader problem remains fuzzy. [research__position_adversarial_ml_for_llms_is_not_making_any_580cc8c8]

**Reverse the demand for a particular formalization** when it excludes the actual deployment hazard or makes irrelevant assumptions convenient. Do not reverse the requirement to state limitations: replace an unsuitable formal claim with a narrower operational one, not an undefined promise of security.

## 5. Treat failed attacks as debugging evidence before treating them as defensive success

**When** a defense survives an optimizer, **investigate the failure mechanism and adapt the attack to the defended computation**. Start with simple, interpretable objectives; add complexity when diagnosis shows why it is needed.

This pattern begins well before language models. Defensive-distillation work traced apparent protection to logit scaling and vanishing gradients, then repaired attack calculations. Obfuscated Gradients differentiated nondifferentiability, stochasticity, and unstable gradients rather than prescribing one universal workaround. [research__defensive_distillation_is_not_robust_to_adversaria_c12d9770] [research__obfuscated_gradients_give_a_false_sense_of_securit_50db64a2]

Sabre is especially diagnostic. Carlini first removed an unnecessary backward-pass approximation around differentiable preprocessing; after the implementation changed, he inserted an approximation where rounding made it useful. The rule is therefore not “use BPDA,” but “make optimization reflect the actual forward behavior.” [research__cutting_through_buggy_adversarial_example_defenses_11b2d112]

Escalation should follow evidence: inspect loss trajectories, retain successful intermediate iterates, test restarts and stronger budgets, compare gradient-free or transfer results, and construct known-vulnerable positive controls. Known-solution tests can expose incompetent attacks without proving that the original defense is ineffective. [research__indicators_of_attack_failure_debugging_and_improvi_f7e3a5ff] [research__increasing_confidence_in_adversarial_robustness_ev_fa33619a]

**Competing priority:** a weak attack can already falsify a strong claim. Carlini’s AmI evaluation deliberately avoided difficult Caffe integration because repeated simpler candidates sufficed; that does not make weak attacks adequate for supporting security. [research__is_ami_attacks_meet_interpretability_robust_to_adv_28a9e78f]

**Reverse escalation** when a valid counterexample already answers the question and additional optimization adds little knowledge. Conversely, certification, strong positive controls, and independent convergent evaluations should increase confidence—but no finite collection of failed attacks establishes unrestricted robustness.

## 10. Model move order, persistence, and the resources attackers actually need

**When** protection depends on remaining effective after release, **ask who commits first, who can adapt later, and which costs recur**.

Facial-recognition protections and artist-protection perturbations share a temporal asymmetry: already-collected images remain fixed while future trainers can change preprocessing or models. Present effectiveness therefore cannot establish durable protection. This is neither opposition to privacy nor endorsement of unauthorized mimicry; it is a warning about relying on an irreversible release. [research__2106_14851_data_poisoning_won_t_save_you_from_faci_ef496c92] [research__adversarial_perturbations_cannot_reliably_protect__512de461]

Poisoning can reverse that order: an attacker commits documents before defenders inspect data or perform later training. Persistence studies accordingly evaluate post-training, finding some objectives survive while jailbreak effects fail or degrade. Fixed-count poisoning research challenges percentage-only accounting, but its near-constant result remains conditional on ordering, learning rates, objectives, and subsequent clean training. [research__persistent_pre_training_poisoning_of_llms_589bcd14] [research__poisoning_attacks_on_llms_require_a_near_constant__553173eb]

Likewise, total query count may misstate attack cost if flagged queries trigger sanctions. **Exception:** a short-lived mitigation can be worthwhile when its lifetime matches the application.

**Reverse a durability warning** only with evidence covering later transformations or enforceable constraints on future access—not another test against today’s default model. Reverse an economic conclusion when realistic account, compute, verification, or retry costs change the attacker’s feasible strategy.

## Resolving conflicts among the rules

These rules imply a conditional ordering, not one maxim:

1. **Validity overrides speed at the claim boundary.** Cheap prototypes and cached environments are appropriate for exploration; independent replay, correct threat constraints, and defensible uncertainty govern publication. Long computation is justified when it changes the inference.
2. **The research objective determines the adversary.** Worst-case claims require adaptive search. Practical protection may instead be judged by enforceable costs and retained utility. Neither standard should silently replace the other.
3. **Concrete harm can justify action before comprehensive theory.** An operational vulnerability may warrant disclosure without a complete formal account. Conversely, a laboratory mechanism should not be promoted into demonstrated deployment compromise.
4. **Openness yields to specific comparative-harm reasons, not institutional discomfort.** Separate warning from weaponization, and distinguish private remediation from indefinite suppression.
5. **Constructive progress and negative evidence coexist.** A broken evaluation need not invalidate the underlying idea; a useful mitigation need not establish security. Revisit defenses when better controls or materially changed systems warrant it.
6. **Purpose governs stopping.** A hobby artifact may stop at satisfying functionality; a scientific project may stop after a decisive counterexample; a deployment decision may require broader validation. Further work is warranted when unresolved uncertainty changes the consequential decision—not merely because additional experiments are possible.

The most stable inferred pattern is therefore: make the claim precise, seek a discriminating test, inspect the mechanism and implementation, preserve inconvenient evidence, and choose the next action according to what the result actually establishes.
