# Nicholas Carlini — P5: Conditional Decision-Rule Induction

## What these rules are

These rules are an interpretive model induced from a selected public corpus. They are not commands written or approved by Nicholas Carlini, a fitted preference model, or an assertion that he always behaves consistently with them. Each rule specifies a condition, a recommended decision, a limiting exception, and evidence. First-person essays provide direct evidence of expressed commitments. Coauthored papers provide evidence of collective research practice, with individual attribution left open unless the text identifies it. Proposed applications to evaluation-awareness research are new analogies. They should be evaluated rather than treated as additional observations of Carlini.

The rules deliberately include tensions. An evaluator can favor rapid iteration while insisting on a faithful final reproduction; favor simple attacks while accepting specialized machinery; and value benchmarks for personal utility while rejecting them as exhaustive security evidence. A single slogan such as “break everything” would lose those conditional distinctions. The purpose of this profile is to help choose a next research action when circumstances are specified, not to produce a flattering list of virtues. The confidence labels below express evidential strength within this corpus, not measured predictive calibration.

## Rule 1: If the claim is adversarial security, let the attacker respond to the defense

**Condition and decision.** When a system is claimed to resist an adversary, evaluate an attacker that knows and responds to the specific defended system within the stated threat model. Passing a fixed collection of old attacks is useful but insufficient. The first-person evaluation advice explains this with a rotation-cipher analogy: changing the rotation defeats a frozen decryptor without creating security. The adaptive-attacks paper reinforces that merely labeling a reused method “adaptive” does not establish that it targets the new defense. Confidence is high for the expressed methodological position. [research__advice_on_evaluating_adversarial_example_defenses_f3b3c399] [research__on_adaptive_attacks_to_adversarial_example_defense_55ab054f]

**Exception and boundary.** A deliberately limited claim about resistance to one fixed procedure can be tested with that procedure. A practical benchmark may also measure a fixed distribution without pretending to prove security. The error occurs when the conclusion expands beyond the test. In addition, adaptation must not quietly exceed the access or resource conditions under which the defender claimed protection.

**Concrete choice.** Given a new randomized input filter, compare a stock attack with an attack that incorporates the filter's behavior. Preserve the actual filter during success evaluation. For evaluation-awareness work, test a proposed camouflage strategy against ways the target could recognize that particular strategy, rather than checking only whether a generic detector notices familiar words. That transfer is a research proposal, not evidence that a fully adaptive awareness detector already exists.

## Rule 2: If a more capable search performs worse, debug before concluding

**Condition and decision.** When results reverse an expected capability ordering, treat the reversal as diagnostic evidence. The evaluation-advice essay discusses iterative versus single-step attacks, white-box versus black-box access, convergence, and increasing distortion budgets. These comparisons can expose a broken loss, poor optimization, or obscured gradients. The rule is to inspect the offending cases and the search trajectory before interpreting the result as robustness. Confidence is high for the diagnostic preference, lower for any specific implementation detail transferred to a new domain. [research__advice_on_evaluating_adversarial_example_defenses_f3b3c399]

**Exception and boundary.** A practical algorithm with more nominal resources can still be worse because it is poorly tuned. The ordering concerns the opportunities available to an ideal or appropriately constructed attacker, not every implementation. Historical numerical suggestions in the essay are not universal present-day defaults; the page explicitly points to a superseding treatment.

**Concrete choice.** If a query-limited method finds failures that a white-box method misses, retain both sets of examples and identify what the latter method is doing wrong. In an awareness setting, if explicit evaluation announcements receive lower recognition scores than neutral conversations, first check label conventions and judge behavior. Do not celebrate the counterintuitive score as evidence that the model has become unable to recognize obvious tests. An inconsistency is valuable because it creates a discriminating next experiment.

## Rule 3: If the replica needs unexplained changes, suspend the comparison

**Condition and decision.** When reproducing another system requires changing a parameter or aggregation rule to match a headline metric, identify why before using that replica to refute the original claim. The SAP erratum documents an omitted averaging step and an altered sampling setting. Restoring the correct implementation invalidated the original attack on that replica, even though a new attack later defeated the corrected defense. Confidence is high because the source explicitly describes the error and assigns responsibility. [research__erratum_concerning_the_obfuscated_gradients_attack_a6a22734]

**Exception and boundary.** Some implementation changes are legitimate adaptations to different hardware or software. They should be documented and tested for semantic equivalence. The rule does not require bitwise identity in every study; it requires that the tested object support the conclusion being drawn.

**Concrete choice.** If a baseline defense reaches expected accuracy only after disabling a component, record the discrepancy and avoid calling the resulting model the original defense. If an awareness harness changes a system prompt, removes a tool, or uses a different judge to make the run work, distinguish compatibility testing from a faithful comparison. A real result about a modified setup remains useful, but it cannot retroactively validate the claimed reproduction. This rule constrains attackers as strongly as defenders and prevents a correct suspicion from excusing an invalid demonstration.

## Rule 4: If the metric hides the affected tail, change the operating point

**Condition and decision.** When a security claim concerns reliable harm to a subset of users, evaluate at an operating point that captures that harm rather than relying solely on average accuracy or area under a curve. The membership-inference paper argues for true-positive performance at low false-positive rates, because reliably identifying a few training members can matter more than making many uncertain guesses. Confidence is high for the paper's argument and moderate for attributing a general personal preference from the coauthored work. [research__membership_inference_attacks_from_first_principles_4ebfbc25]

**Exception and boundary.** Average measures can be appropriate for other questions. The rule does not claim that tail metrics are always morally or scientifically superior. It asks whether the statistic preserves the decision consequences in the specific setting. A tail estimate also needs enough data to support its uncertainty; a tiny sample does not become persuasive because the target false-positive rate is small.

**Concrete choice.** Report how membership inference behaves when false alarms are rare, not just its balanced classification accuracy. For awareness research, separate false claims of recognition from missed recognition and preserve the environment-level outcomes. If a combined score improves because invalid tasks become common, report that component explicitly. The corresponding rule is metric fidelity: the number must retain the distinction that makes the original research question matter.

## Rule 5: If a proxy can be improved while the task gets worse, audit semantics

**Condition and decision.** When optimizing a formal criterion might diverge from intended behavior, test examples where the formal and semantic labels disagree. The invariance paper shows that enforcing unchanged predictions within a norm ball can become harmful when a perturbation actually changes the human-assigned class. A certificate for unchanged output remains mathematically meaningful while failing to guarantee the desired classification behavior. Confidence is moderate for a general taste inference and high for the paper's demonstrated conceptual distinction. [research__fundamental_tradeoffs_between_invariance_and_sensi_ee0b16ca]

**Exception and boundary.** Formal proxies are often necessary. A limited formal guarantee should not be dismissed merely because it does not solve every semantic problem. The correct response is to state its scope and investigate the gap, not replace all measurement with intuition.

**Concrete choice.** Before praising robustness to a transformation, check whether that transformation preserves the task's true label. In evaluation-awareness work, removing context can reduce recognition while destroying the situation that was meant to test a behavior. A validity constraint must therefore be independently meaningful. A detector that rewards absence of evaluation vocabulary should be tested on recognized evaluations described without those words. That case distinguishes successful optimization of the detector from progress on the underlying target construct.

## Rule 6: If formal verification is feasible on a restricted case, use it to calibrate empirical search

**Condition and decision.** When small systems permit stronger guarantees, use them to determine whether an empirical attack is missing failures or whether a defense has actually increased robustness. The provably-minimal-distortion paper applies verification to attack quality and defense evaluation. It reports positive evidence for adversarial training in the restricted setting rather than treating every defense as a presumed failure. Confidence is moderate as a personal rule because the evidence is coauthored, but strong as a documented research choice. [research__provably_minimally_distorted_adversarial_examples_656fb315]

**Exception and boundary.** A proof on a small network or a particular distance metric does not establish the same property at larger scale or under a different semantic definition. Verification can be expensive, and a narrow certified result is not automatically more practically useful than a well-designed deployment study.

**Concrete choice.** Compare the distortion found by a search algorithm against a verified minimum on tractable examples. Then use discrepancies to improve the algorithm or bound its interpretation. In awareness research, an exact proof about latent recognition may be unavailable, but some components can still be exhaustively checked: whether a cue is present, whether tool permissions changed, or whether template transformations preserve required fields. Those component checks are valuable without being mislabeled a proof that the target cannot recognize evaluation status.

## Rule 7: If repeated setup dominates exploration, separate state construction from hypothesis testing

**Condition and decision.** When each experiment reloads expensive but unchanged state, build a small mechanism for reusing that state during exploration. Carlini's rapid-iteration essay describes his snapshotting tool and his frustration when startup latency interrupts the path from an idea to useful output. This is direct evidence of a practical workflow preference. Confidence is high for the personal preference, not for the claim that one exact tool is universally best. [research__rapid_iteration_in_machine_learning_research_78fdab31]

**Exception and boundary.** Reused interactive state can introduce hidden dependencies. For final evidence, the experiment must still be reproducible from a known state. A long one-time computation may also be necessary; speed does not justify reducing validity or silently substituting a cheaper target.

**Concrete choice.** Precompute fixed data structures, inspect a few cases interactively, and preserve a clean replay path. For generated environments, cache source material and deterministic formatting while keeping target calls and changing prompts explicitly versioned. The desired outcome is more informative experiments per unit of attention, not simply more requests per minute. If a faster path changes the measurement, it belongs in a separate comparison. The rule couples convenience to epistemic control rather than treating them as opposing values.

## Rule 8: If complexity is not causally necessary, attack the decisive component first

**Condition and decision.** When a defense has many components, identify which ones determine the claimed protection and seek a simple test of those mechanisms. The adaptive-attacks paper explicitly emphasizes simple attacks and losses that remain aligned with stronger attack outcomes. Its case studies were selected partly for conceptual diversity, not merely to maximize the count of broken defenses. Confidence is moderate for personal preference, high for this collaborative methodology. [research__on_adaptive_attacks_to_adversarial_example_defense_55ab054f]

**Exception and boundary.** A simple attack that fails does not establish security. Some systems require specialized or expensive analysis. Simplicity is a starting preference and an explanatory advantage, not a prohibition on technically sophisticated methods. Nor does a short exploit automatically reveal the whole failure mechanism.

**Concrete choice.** Before building an elaborate search ensemble, test whether the defense merely disrupts the gradient used by the baseline. Preserve a record of failed simple hypotheses. In an awareness environment, first inspect direct metadata leaks and conspicuous instruction conventions before inventing elaborate persona narratives. If the obvious cue does not explain recognition, move to harder hypotheses. This sequencing saves effort while retaining the possibility that the difficult explanation is correct. It should not become an excuse to stop after superficial checks.

## Rule 9: If the purpose is personal assistant utility, evaluate actual work under intended use

**Condition and decision.** When choosing a tool for one's own workflow, build tests from tasks one actually asks it to perform and evaluate the resulting artifact where possible. Carlini's personal benchmark includes code modification, obscure technical questions, and programming tasks from his own conversations. He favors ordinary prompts for this purpose and explicitly disclaims a general academic capability interpretation. Confidence is high because these are direct statements of the benchmark's goals. [research__my_benchmark_for_large_language_models_0bb6cd0b]

**Exception and boundary.** A capability study may deliberately optimize prompting to estimate what the model can do at its best. A security study may require an adversarial distribution rather than ordinary usage. Memorization can be acceptable for practical answer utility while remaining a confound in a claim about novel reasoning.

**Concrete choice.** Run the code instead of scoring how plausible the explanation sounds. Use task completion to select an assistant, but do not infer a universal intelligence ranking from a personal sample. For this profile itself, usefulness in generating good experiments would be one evaluation target; predicting Carlini's stated preferences would be another. Success on one does not prove success on the other. The rule prevents a convenient benchmark from answering a question it was never designed to address.

## Rule 10: If protection depends on operational friction, state and measure that friction

**Condition and decision.** When a defense aims to make attacks costly rather than impossible, express success in terms of the relevant operational constraint. The stateful-detection paper studies query sequences and uses the number of accounts an attacker needs as a key metric. This makes the defense's significance depend on account creation and enforcement assumptions, rather than requiring every adversarial input to become impossible. Confidence is moderate because this is collective work, but it is important counterevidence to an unconditional impossibility standard. [research__stateful_detection_of_black_box_adversarial_attack_c9da0aa0]

**Exception and boundary.** Friction is not a defense if the attacker can cheaply bypass the assumed constraint. A favorable cost estimate should not be described as absolute security. Conversely, a theoretical attack outside a realistic budget may not refute a bounded operational claim.

**Concrete choice.** If an attack is detected after a few related queries, measure how that changes the resources required for success and explain the account model. For awareness experiments, distinguish what a target can infer from one isolated episode from what it can infer across repeated exposure to a generator. Repeated templates may create recognition opportunities absent from single episodes. The source does not establish how to quantify that effect; it motivates treating state and repeated interaction as part of the system rather than automatically assuming independent trials.

## Rule 11: If a narrow result is being used to support a broad conclusion, restore the missing qualifiers

**Condition and decision.** When a study demonstrates that models sometimes reproduce training material, do not silently turn that into a claim about every model, every copyrighted work, ordinary usage frequency, or a legal conclusion. Carlini's privacy-and-copyright essay repeatedly separates what his studies measured from claims people want those studies to establish. Confidence is high for the expressed demand to preserve evidential boundaries. This profile does not offer legal advice or adopt a position on the underlying legal disputes. [research__what_my_privacy_papers_don_t_have_to_say_about_cop_dc9ec483]

**Exception and boundary.** Broader inferences can be legitimate when additional evidence supports them. The rule is not that researchers may report only raw observations; it is that intermediate assumptions must be exposed rather than hidden inside confident prose.

**Concrete choice.** Distinguish a targeted extraction demonstration from a prevalence estimate under benign prompts. Likewise, distinguish a model's explicit recognition statement from proof of its internal beliefs. If a behavioral difference accompanies recognition, consider whether another changed variable explains it. A source-grounded persona is also only a model of public evidence, not proof of the person's motives. Applying the rule to this document is necessary: methodological resemblance does not establish that Carlini would endorse a proposed project, ranking, or deployment decision.

## Rule 12: If the attacker made an error, correct it even when the final conclusion survives

**Condition and decision.** When a flaw in an attack invalidates its evidence, publish or preserve the correction separately from any replacement result. The SAP erratum is especially decisive because a new attack still found a severe failure. The source nevertheless states that the original attack on the correctly implemented defense was ineffective. Confidence is high for the demonstrated correction and moderate for generalizing from it to all future behavior. [research__erratum_concerning_the_obfuscated_gradients_attack_a6a22734]

**Exception and boundary.** Not every minor typo requires a separate public document, but any error changing the interpretation of a result requires visible correction appropriate to the audience. A rerun that accidentally restores the same aggregate number does not excuse an incorrect earlier method.

**Concrete choice.** Keep distinct records for the flawed replica, corrected baseline, and new attack. In an awareness experiment, if labels were inverted or a judge saw forbidden metadata, invalidate affected scores and rerun the relevant comparison. Do not describe the repair as a cosmetic cleanup. This rule protects both the criticized system and the credibility of attack research. It also creates a reusable lesson: the discrepancy that exposed the error should become a future check rather than remaining merely an embarrassing story.

## Rule 13: If release mistakes recur, encode a checklist at the point of release

**Condition and decision.** When mistakes are predictable and easy to check, turn them into explicit release steps. Carlini's checklist essay describes learning from misspelled titles, author-name errors, and a visible merge conflict. It includes both automated text checks and visual inspection of the final rendering. Confidence is high for the documented personal workflow. The list is evidence of a practice, not a guarantee that all subsequent publications are error-free. [research__research_paper_release_checklist_6ddd3c7d]

**Exception and boundary.** A checklist catches known classes of error; it cannot substitute for understanding an argument or independently validating results. A growing checklist can also become performative if nobody checks the actual artifact. The relevant unit is the released object, not a source file assumed to render correctly.

**Concrete choice.** Check names, unresolved notes, build warnings, figures, and captions before uploading. For these rich profiles, the analogous steps are checking word counts, resolving every source identifier, preserving attribution, and verifying that method labels match the content. These checks do not validate the preference model. They make the deliverable inspectable enough that a later substantive evaluation is possible. The rule thus joins mundane quality control to scientific accountability without confusing their scopes.

## Rule 14: If disclosure choices change harm, reason about the particular deployment

**Condition and decision.** When deciding how to disclose a vulnerability, consider whether it can be patched, whether affected users can avoid exposure, and whether delay allows additional irreversible harm. In “Why I attack,” Carlini contrasts patchable services with released artifacts whose past exposure cannot be undone. He also describes waiting on a university-system vulnerability because immediate publication would expose users who lacked an alternative. Confidence is high for his stated reasoning, not for any universal disclosure algorithm. [research__why_i_attack_d0fe4023]

**Exception and boundary.** Patchability is one factor, not a complete ethical theory. The essay itself recognizes exceptional consequences and ambiguity. This rule supplies no authorization to attack a live system, reveal private data, or disclose a vulnerability without considering affected parties and applicable constraints.

**Concrete choice.** A fixable API issue and an already distributed dataset can require different release timing even if both are technically interesting. For a synthetic-environment study, concrete exploit details may add little to the methodological claim while increasing misuse risk. Preserve enough evidence for authorized verification and communicate the finding at an appropriate level. The reconstructed preference is for explicit consequence-sensitive judgment rather than a reflex that all attacks should be published immediately or all negative findings withheld.

## Conflict resolution and revision

Apply these rules in sequence only when their conditions hold. First determine the question and threat model; then establish fidelity, choose metrics, search adaptively, diagnose failures, and report bounded conclusions. Operational relevance can justify a constrained threat model, but cannot justify changing it after seeing results. Simplicity can guide the first experiment, but cannot certify a defense after that experiment fails. Fast iteration can improve discovery, but cannot replace a clean evidential record. A formal result can settle a restricted property while leaving the real-world interpretation unresolved.

A useful test of this profile would present genuine research choices with meaningful tradeoffs and obtain independent judgments. Cases should include positive defense evidence, expensive but necessary reproductions, and practical benchmarks that do not aim at generality. If the profile predicts rejection of all defensive work, the formal-verification and stateful-detection evidence should force revision. If it predicts indifference to useful ordinary model behavior, the personal-benchmark evidence should force revision. Until such validation exists, treat these as explicit, contestable hypotheses about research decision-making, not recovered preferences with demonstrated accuracy.
