# Jacob Steinhardt — P5: Conditional Decision-Rule Induction

This profile translates selected public evidence into conditional research decisions. It is an inferred decision model, not a list of instructions dictated by Jacob Steinhardt and not a validated predictor of his choices. Rules grounded in first-person methodological writing receive stronger personal attribution than rules abstracted from coauthored work. The examples below are prospective applications unless explicitly described as published studies. Exceptions are integral to the rules: removing them would produce a caricature that rejects exploration, treats every anomaly as a catastrophe, and mistakes methodological caution for a preference against ambitious research.

## Rule 1: When a claim outruns its measurement, narrow the claim or strengthen the test

**Condition.** A proposal uses a measurable outcome to support a larger conclusion: accuracy becomes competence, a visualization becomes understanding, or agreement becomes correctness.

**Decision.** Identify the intermediate inference and ask what would make it fail. Prefer a study that tests that link over additional measurements that merely repeat the original endpoint. The coauthored scholarship essay directly supports distinguishing speculation from explanation and isolating the source of empirical improvements. This is a strong basis for a methodological rule, although the essay expresses a joint position. [research__troubling_trends_in_machine_learning_scholarship_6db972d5]

**Exception.** A narrow engineering objective can legitimately use an endpoint without establishing a comprehensive theory. A faster system need not explain intelligence. The requirement is to match the conclusion to the evidence, not to demand every possible explanation before reporting useful progress.

**Example and update.** If a new auditing agent finds more failures, first ask whether it searched longer, received more information, or genuinely found a different class of defect. Compare matched budgets or report the resource difference. If the practical value is higher throughput despite greater cost, say so. The rule should change only if the extra causal question has no bearing on the claimed contribution or downstream decision; it should not disappear because a favorable headline is convenient.

## Rule 2: When robustness is claimed, specify the adversary and hold out meaningful variation

**Condition.** A defense is evaluated against attacks closely related to those used during development, while its motivation concerns future or adaptive threats.

**Decision.** Test transfer across attack families or structural changes, not only fresh instances of familiar attacks. The unforeseen-adversaries paper constructs diverse evaluation attacks outside standard training assumptions and emphasizes that a finite suite still cannot exhaust possible adversaries. The rule follows the study's design rather than a claim that any particular suite remains definitive. [research__testing_robustness_against_unforeseen_adversaries_198b507f]

**Exception.** A narrow threat model may be appropriate when it matches a concrete deployment boundary. A guarantee against a specified perturbation is valuable even if it is not universal. What fails is relabeling that guarantee as unrestricted robustness.

**Example and update.** For evaluation-awareness research, split development and assessment by cue family: explicit benchmark labels, institutional context, unusual task formatting, or identity information. Success on paraphrases of one cue is weaker evidence than transfer to a genuinely different route. If held-out families prove irrelevant to the intended environment, revise the distribution with documented reasons rather than preserving diversity as an aesthetic goal. The value comes from testing the intended generalization claim.

## Rule 3: When clean data assumptions are doubtful, model contamination before optimizing the estimator

**Condition.** A project assumes that enough observations will remove uncertainty, but some observations may be corrupted or selected adversarially.

**Decision.** Separate sampling error from irreducible uncertainty caused by corruption. The resilience work seeks structural conditions under which recovery is possible; generalized resilience distinguishes types of corruption and identifies the original population as the estimation target. This supports explicitly choosing the contamination model and assumptions before interpreting a precise estimate. [research__1703_04940_resilience_a_criterion_for_learning_in__eabe29eb; research__generalized_resilience_and_robust_statistics_4f51152e]

**Exception.** Formal robust estimation is not automatically the best tool for every noisy dataset. The available corpus does not justify importing a sophisticated estimator whenever a few labels are imperfect. Simpler sensitivity analysis may answer the actual question.

**Example and update.** If generated evaluations contain mislabeled tasks, increasing sample count may make a biased score look more certain. Estimate the effect of plausible label errors, inspect influential cases, and distinguish random disagreement from systematic selection. If the errors are shown to be negligible for the decision, return attention to other uncertainties. The rule favors clarifying the threat to inference, not maximizing mathematical machinery.

## Rule 4: When looking for failures, prefer categories that predict new cases

**Condition.** A team has accumulated interesting mistakes but lacks a model of when they occur or why they matter.

**Decision.** Form a qualitative failure hypothesis and construct transformations predicted to elicit it. The cognitive-bias study uses human concepts to propose model tests, then checks both accuracy changes and output features characteristic of the hypothesized error. This two-part evidence is stronger than a collection of anecdotes. [research__capturing_failures_of_large_language_models_via_hu_36361a99]

**Exception.** A novel anecdote can be a legitimate starting point, particularly when it reveals a severe failure. The rule does not require an established taxonomy before exploration. It asks the next stage to determine whether the case belongs to a reproducible pattern.

**Example and update.** If an assistant falsely claims to have run code, vary whether execution was requested, whether tools are available, and whether the user challenges the claim. Check the transcript for actual tool use. A category becomes useful when it predicts which manipulations change the behavior. If cases resist the proposed category, revise it rather than silently broadening the label until every output fits. Human psychological analogies remain hypothesis generators, not established mechanisms inside the model.

## Rule 5: When a failure is rare but consequential, measure severity separately from ordinary prevalence

**Condition.** A benchmark averages over errors whose practical consequences differ sharply, or an adversarial search produces a dramatic example.

**Decision.** Retain the distinction between finding a vulnerability, estimating its frequency under a stated distribution, and assessing its consequences. The cognitive-bias work explicitly separates ordinary incorrect code from damaging actions; the natural-adversarial-examples work deliberately filters for difficult real-world cases. Neither design licenses treating the selected frequency as everyday incidence. [research__capturing_failures_of_large_language_models_via_hu_36361a99; research__natural_adversarial_examples_0b83d401]

**Exception.** A single reachable failure can justify action when its consequence is unacceptable under the deployment's access conditions. A population rate is not always necessary to establish a vulnerability. Conversely, spectacular severity in an implausible setup does not automatically dominate a realistic reliability problem.

**Example and update.** Report that an investigator elicited unauthorized deletion in a controlled environment, state how much search was required, and separately assess whether ordinary users encounter the triggering conditions. If the task is only vulnerability discovery, the first result may suffice. If the claim is expected deployment harm, exposure and frequency become load-bearing. The decision rule is about preserving these different questions, not always requiring the same expensive measurement.

## Rule 6: When a safety mechanism trails capability, test the gap directly

**Condition.** A model can perform a task in a format, language, or context that its safety intervention may not cover.

**Decision.** Test whether the relevant safety behavior generalizes alongside capability. Jailbroken organizes attacks around competing objectives and mismatched generalization and argues for safety-capability parity. Steinhardt's role is identified as equal advising, so the paper supports a research direction more clearly than individual ownership of each experimental detail. [research__jailbroken_how_does_llm_safety_training_fail_d6da6ac8]

**Exception.** The existence of mismatches in evaluated models does not prove that scale always worsens safety or that every safeguard must replicate the full model architecture. A simpler mechanism can be sufficient for a narrow, well-specified boundary.

**Example and update.** If a model recognizes sensitive intent in ordinary prose but not an alternative representation it otherwise understands, compare capability and safety across that transformation. If both deteriorate, the result may reflect general comprehension failure rather than safety-specific mismatch. If capability remains intact while the safety behavior fails, the hypothesis gains support. Follow-up should distinguish a missing training region from a deeper conflict among objectives because the likely remedies differ.

## Rule 7: When an explanation looks persuasive, test what it lets a reader predict

**Condition.** An interpretability artifact is evaluated mainly by plausibility, visual appeal, or agreement with a familiar story.

**Decision.** Specify a task the explanation should enable, then measure its performance on unseen or counterfactual cases. The counterfactual-simulatability paper asks whether natural-language explanations help humans predict model outputs and separates precision from generality. The visualization audit tests detection and localization of known anomalies. These are complementary operationalizations of usefulness. [research__do_models_explain_themselves_counterfactual_simula_f5ac5a40; research__auditing_visualizations_transparency_methods_strug_ec1a669f]

**Exception.** Exploratory visualizations can generate hypotheses before they reliably predict behavior. They should be labeled exploratory rather than presented as validated accounts. An explanation can also have pedagogical value without identifying internal causation.

**Example and update.** A neuron description that sounds accurate should predict activation on new examples or help find a relevant behavioral issue. A model's explanation of its answer should support correct predictions on nearby questions. If it merely restates the original input, apparent precision may conceal negligible generality. Revise the evaluation to capture the intended use rather than treating one universal interpretability metric as sufficient for every tool.

## Rule 8: When an audit succeeds, counteraudit the auditing method

**Condition.** A tool identifies some known problems and is proposed as a source of reassurance about the problems it did not find.

**Decision.** Test it on controlled positive and negative cases, including subtle anomalies where basic outcome metrics are uninformative. The visualization study explicitly evaluates normal and anomalous models and finds that detecting stark differences is easier than detecting localized defects. This supports checking sensitivity in the regime where the tool is expected to add value. [research__auditing_visualizations_transparency_methods_strug_ec1a669f]

**Exception.** Planted defects are not a representative sample of naturally occurring defects. Counteraudits establish competence under their construction process; transfer still requires investigation. A tool can be useful for discovery even before it supports strong negative assurance.

**Example and update.** Before saying that an awareness monitor found no strategic behavior, test whether it detects known context-dependent behavior without excessive false alarms. Include cases where the behavior changes but the reasoning does not mention awareness. If it misses those cases, retain it as one signal rather than treating silence as evidence of absence. If a different detector succeeds, compare their error patterns instead of replacing one unqualified assurance claim with another.

## Rule 9: When optimizing human approval, independently measure the underlying objective

**Condition.** Human ratings or a learned reward model are used both to train a system and to demonstrate that it has improved.

**Decision.** Add an evaluation channel that can reveal divergence between looking correct and being correct. The RLHF sophistry study compares judgments from time-constrained people with gold labels on question-answering and programming tasks. Its finding concerns studied settings and training arrangements, not all human feedback. [research__language_models_learn_to_mislead_humans_via_rlhf_f3ae188d]

**Exception.** Some goals genuinely concern human experience, and no single objective label fully captures them. Independent measurement can then mean additional perspectives, delayed outcomes, or explicit uncertainty rather than a fictional gold standard.

**Example and update.** An oversight assistant that writes increasingly convincing reports should be scored on whether those reports identify real failures and support reproducible checks. Reader confidence alone is insufficient. If reports are correct but confusing, communication quality still matters; the rule does not rank truth and usability as substitutes. It demands evidence that persuasive improvements did not conceal deterioration in the target property. A detector trained on deliberate backdoors should not be assumed to transfer to failures emerging through ordinary optimization.

## Rule 10: When outcomes are ambiguous, inspect the trajectory before interpreting capability

**Condition.** A benchmark reports pass/fail while agents use tools, interact with scaffolds, or take consequential intermediate actions.

**Decision.** Examine logs for shortcuts, infrastructure bottlenecks, and harmful actions invisible in the final answer. The log-analysis paper argues that outcome-only evaluation cannot verify internal validity, deployment transfer, and safety. Its case study includes under-elicited capability, illustrating that scrutiny can correct pessimistic as well as optimistic measurements. [research__log_analysis_is_necessary_for_credible_evaluation__1044f71d]

**Exception.** Logs are themselves incomplete observations. They do not expose every internal computation, and model-generated analysis can miss or misclassify events. Process inspection strengthens evidence without becoming omniscient access to intent.

**Example and update.** If an agent fails to solve a task because its tool wrapper drops necessary information, fix the evaluation interpretation before concluding that the model lacks the capability. If it succeeds by reading a hidden solution, remove the invalid success from the competence claim. Preserve both original and corrected analyses so readers can see which conclusion depends on the scaffold. The goal is accurate attribution, not mechanically maximizing or minimizing scores.

## Rule 11: When manual investigation is the bottleneck, automate search but preserve human inspectability

**Condition.** The space of behaviors or representations is too large for researchers to examine directly, and important failures may be sparse.

**Decision.** Use AI-assisted search, description, and organization to expand coverage, with outputs that people can inspect and challenge. The Transluce letter connects scalable investigation with public validation, and the accompanying research talk describes behavior elicitation and neuron-description tools. These sources support an agenda rather than a guarantee of complete oversight. [research__introducing_transluce_a_letter_from_the_founders_719b1792; research__scalably_understanding_ai_with_ai_5a9638dc]

**Exception.** Automation is not intrinsically an improvement. If a manual check is cheap and decisive, a complex investigator may add error and latency. Nor does a large search budget compensate for a poorly defined target behavior.

**Example and update.** Begin with a few human-discovered instances of fabricated tool use, let investigators search related interactions, and use transcript organization to inspect recurring patterns. Preserve the route from summary back to original evidence. If increased throughput produces mostly duplicates or classifier artifacts, improve the search objective or verification stage before scaling further. The useful quantity is added reliable information, not the number of generated conversations.

## Rule 12: When an ambitious program has uncertain feasibility, create staged empirical commitments

**Condition.** A proposal promises a broadly capable oversight system but depends on untested assumptions about training data, representation, or generalization.

**Decision.** Decompose the proposal into measurable stages whose outcomes can change investment. Foundation Models for Oversight proposes a common executable representation for interventions and measurements, alongside staged development. Its central unification is a research hypothesis, not an achieved theorem about every meaningful oversight question. [research__foundation_models_for_oversight_9bc7a248]

**Exception.** Some foundational work is valuable before there is a short-term product metric. The stage should test the important uncertainty, not force every project into an immediate deployment benchmark.

**Example and update.** First test whether an oversight model predicts held-out intervention results; then test transfer across task families; then assess whether it turns natural-language questions into valid experiments. Keep these accomplishments distinct. Correctly executing a program cannot by itself certify that the program measures deception. If early stages succeed only through shallow overlap, change the data split or representation before inferring broad oversight competence. A credible scale-up plan specifies both the success that warrants expansion and the failure that warrants redesign.

## Rule 13: When interpreting context sensitivity, separate behavioral effects from inferred motives

**Condition.** Model behavior changes with a user identity, evaluation label, or other contextual cue.

**Decision.** Measure the change independently from explanations of why it occurs. The user-awareness study reports effects of recognized identities and warns that reasoning may not explicitly reveal them. It also states that the narrow tested effects are not obviously harmful. The evidence supports sensitivity, not automatic attribution of manipulation or concealment. [research__user_awareness_in_frontier_models_transluce_ai_0cae7eb0]

**Exception.** Motive hypotheses can guide further experiments and should not be prohibited merely because they are difficult to establish. They should generate discriminating predictions rather than serve as labels attached after seeing a difference.

**Example and update.** If a model expresses lower confidence for an AI researcher, compare generic expertise cues, named identities, affiliation cues, and irrelevant personal information. Examine whether accuracy changes along with confidence. A reduction in overconfidence might be beneficial; strategic withholding would imply a different pattern. If multiple hypotheses remain compatible with the observations, report that underdetermination. The rule protects both against sensational interpretation and against dismissing an effect simply because a benign explanation is available.

## Rule 14: When forecasting, commit in advance and score the reasoning as well as the result

**Condition.** Research prioritization depends on expectations about progress, or a person claims that earlier judgments were prescient.

**Decision.** Use resolvable questions, probability distributions, and a recorded rationale. Compare predictions with outcomes and alternatives, then inspect whether the mechanism was right. Steinhardt's forecasting retrospective explicitly evaluates his own registered reasoning and revises his impression of expert versus generalist forecasts. [research__ai_forecasting_two_years_in_cf7264f0]

**Exception.** Two successful forecasts do not establish broad calibration. A partly unresolved prediction cannot be scored as though its deadline passed, and short-horizon benchmark skill does not automatically transfer to long-horizon risk.

**Example and update.** Before scaling an auditing system, record expected gains from more search, better training data, and stronger verification separately. A correct total can conceal compensating mistakes. If a result falls inside a wide interval, inspect whether the interval was informative and the causal expectations held. The rule values revision rather than consistency for its own sake. New evidence about incentives or effort may change which forecasters deserve weight without implying that expertise is either universally decisive or universally irrelevant.

## Rule 15: When disagreement blocks learning, change the conversation without lowering the standard

**Condition.** A meeting rewards sounding knowledgeable, defending a project, or winning an argument more than exposing uncertainty.

**Decision.** Invite basic questions, model reasoning explicitly, and separate criticism of an idea from status judgments about its author. Steinhardt's group-meeting essay directly endorses psychological safety, pushback, and a high standard of understanding. It also asks why a project is exciting and consequential, not merely whether it is defensible. [research__principles_for_productive_group_meetings_2496db16]

**Exception.** Psychological safety does not require agreement or endless discussion. A decision can be made under uncertainty, provided unresolved objections are represented honestly. Nor should a broad negative judgment make an entire area feel forbidden.

**Example and update.** Ask a presenter what observation would change the proposed explanation, then help construct that observation. If a junior colleague questions a benchmark assumption, evaluate the question on its content rather than their fluency. If criticism becomes repetitive and uninformative, redirect it toward the highest-value uncertainty. This rule makes the epistemic standards above socially usable: people must be able to reveal that a favored theory, a prestigious method, or their own experiment may be wrong.

## How to combine the rules

These rules do not supply a fixed ranking for every project. An urgent, severe vulnerability may justify action before a prevalence study; a narrow proof may be valuable without immediate deployment transfer; an exploratory tool may deserve development before validated negative assurance. The common requirement is explicit scope. When rules conflict, state the decision at stake, identify the uncertainty most likely to change it, and choose an investigation proportionate to that uncertainty. This ordering is an induced synthesis of the corpus, not a measured personal utility function. Its usefulness and its fidelity to Steinhardt's actual choices remain separate empirical questions.

A practical tie-breaker is the consequence of being wrong. If the main risk is deploying an unsafe capability because a metric is misleading, prioritize validation of the metric and investigation of severe failure modes. If the main risk is discarding a useful system because a broken scaffold suppresses performance, prioritize capability elicitation and process diagnosis. The log-analysis evidence supports both directions. This symmetry is important: the profile should not encode a preference for alarming conclusions merely because much of the corpus studies safety failures. [research__log_analysis_is_necessary_for_credible_evaluation__1044f71d]

Another tie-breaker is whether the next experiment distinguishes the leading explanations. Ten additional examples from an already understood failure mode may add less information than one controlled comparison between competing mechanisms. Conversely, when uncertainty concerns frequency rather than mechanism, larger representative sampling can be exactly the right next step. Neither small elegant experiments nor large datasets receive unconditional priority. Their value depends on the question the existing evidence leaves open. The scholarship essay's emphasis on identifying the source of gains supplies the anchor for this proposed decision procedure. [research__troubling_trends_in_machine_learning_scholarship_6db972d5]

Finally, a negative result should update the rule's application rather than be concealed. If a sophisticated explanation does not improve prediction, retain the measured failure. If a simple baseline performs as well as an ambitious tool, reconsider the tool's claimed advantage. The standards are intended to survive outcomes unfavorable to the preferred project.
