HIS COMMENT:
New [study](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/26/8/20-1274_article) from South Korea of spread in a crowded call center. There were 94 infections on one floor (43% of workers on the floor). As most people had symptom onset during a three day period, this suggests 1-2 people were superspreaders. They have a seating [chart](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/26/8/20-1274-f2), which suggests the secondary attack rate was significantly higher for people sitting in the same room (eyeballing maybe 60%). It's notable that some people don't get infected, despite spending 4-5 days full workdays being exposed to a superspreader and possibly other infectious people. Only 4% were asymptomatic for the whole period of the study.

They tested households of the infected office workers and get a household secondary attack rate of 16%. How much were people trying to avoid infecting their families? It's hard to say from the study, but we know the following:

1\. This was around the peak of cases in South Korea. People would be primed to take Covid-like symptoms seriously.

2\. After a few days where many workers developed symptoms, the office was closed. At this point, it seems very likely that most workers took efforts to isolate from their families.

3\. 72% of subjects are women, with mean age 38. It [seems](http://koreajoongangdaily.joins.com/news/article/article.aspx?aid=3003635) that having roommates is relatively rare among Koreans. I'd guess these are nearly all people living older parents and nuclear families. (It's easier for someone to isolate from their parents or older children than from spouse or young kids).

4\. From other [studies](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2006100), under 18s are less likely than adults to get secondary infections and the number is very low for under 10s. It's not clear whether children were tested, but they list 2.3 household contacts per person, which suggests they are. If 1/5 of contacts were younger children, and we removed them, you'd get a secondary attack rate of ~20%.

So what about roommates living together? I'd guess:

1\. If people are fairly sensitive to Covid symptoms and make some efforts to isolate, 15-25% secondary attack rate.

2\. If people don't make any effort to isolate after onset of symptoms, 20-40%.

The spread in the call center and other studies of choirs/restaurants suggest that direct physical contact is not necessary for very effective spread. So roommates spending time together in common spaces would be at high risk.
