How this researcher's choices differ from what a typical strong ML researcher would do. The pattern is general: across many kinds of research decisions, he often departs from the typical choice, and in particular from the most cautious choice that a careful reviewer would recommend by default. The notes below describe that tendency in general terms; they are not tied to any particular project, topic or method.

1. **When choosing which question to study, he often does not pick the question most researchers in the area would pick.**
   - Generic default: Pick the question that the field currently treats as the obvious next step, framed the way most papers in the area frame it.
   - His choice: Frequently a question other than the obvious next step, or the obvious question framed in a way most colleagues would not choose first.

2. **When designing an experiment, he often departs from the standard design.**
   - Generic default: Use the design that a typical strong researcher would consider safest, with the setup reviewers expect to see.
   - His choice: Often a design that differs from the standard one in some respect, even when the standard design is available and would be accepted without comment.

3. **When deciding on controls, baselines and comparisons, he often does not follow the most cautious recommendation.**
   - Generic default: Include whichever set of controls and comparisons a careful reviewer would ask for by default.
   - His choice: Often a set that differs from the default one, chosen on his own judgement rather than on what a reviewer would list.

4. **When choosing how to measure an effect, he often departs from the conventional measurement.**
   - Generic default: Use the metric, threshold and procedure that most papers in the area use, so results are easy to compare.
   - His choice: Frequently a measurement choice other than the conventional one.

5. **When analysing results, he often takes a different route from the typical analysis.**
   - Generic default: Run the analysis a typical strong researcher would run first, in the order a methods section would usually present it.
   - His choice: Often an analysis, ordering or emphasis that differs from that typical route.

6. **When deciding the scope of a project, he often departs from the scope most researchers would settle on.**
   - Generic default: Settle on the scope that a careful planner would consider appropriate for the evidence and resources available.
   - His choice: Frequently a scope that differs from what a careful planner would pick, in one direction or another.

7. **When deciding how strongly to state a conclusion, he often does not choose the most cautious wording.**
   - Generic default: Phrase the conclusion in the way a careful reviewer would consider the safest reading of the evidence.
   - His choice: Often a phrasing other than the safest default reading.

8. **When deciding what to do next after a result, he often does not take the step most colleagues would expect.**
   - Generic default: Take the follow-up step that the field would regard as the natural continuation.
   - His choice: Often a next step other than the natural continuation.

9. **When several reasonable options are on the table, he is less likely than a typical researcher to end up on the conventional one.**
   - Generic default: Among reasonable options, choose the one that the typical strong researcher, and a cautious reviewer, would most likely recommend.
   - His choice: More often than a typical researcher, one of the other reasonable options.

In short: if you can tell which option a typical strong researcher, or the most cautious reviewer, would pick, that is the option he is somewhat less likely to pick than such a researcher would be. This is a tendency, not a rule, and it says nothing about the direction in which he departs; it only says that he departs from the default more often than most researchers do.
