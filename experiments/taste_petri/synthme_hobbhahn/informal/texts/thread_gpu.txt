 We built a composite model to predict FLOP/s of GPUs from their features. We started with >20 features but found that 2 features (the number of cores and the size of transistors) explain 95% of the variance in historical data. 2/n 

 We first predict a distribution over the limit of miniaturization. The relevant literature suggests that the limit lies somewhere between 3.0nm and 0.7nm. For context, current transistors are ~6nm and the size of a silicon atom is ~0.5nm. 3/n 

 Secondly, we assume that once transistor miniaturization hits its limit GPU producers will still try to leverage additional parallelism by adding additional cores, though this too has a limit. 4/n

 Since each core needs a certain amount of transistors, the approach of building massively-multi-core GPUs will run out of steam a few years after lithographic scaling comes to a halt. 5/n 

 Our model suggests that the top GPU performance will plateau somewhere between 1e14 and 1e15 FLOP/s. This is roughly 2 to 20x the current performance of current state-of-the-art GPUs for FP32. 6/n 

 Our model makes a lot of simplifying assumptions and we expect some clever ways to increase performance, such as 3D chip stacking, as well as architectural improvements. However, we think that limits to miniaturization will pose a significant hurdle for hardware producers. 7/n
