# Three-method rich researcher-profile demo

## Intent

Build a hackathon demo that shows three meaningfully different ways to turn a public research record into a rich, source-grounded researcher profile. The initial researchers are Evan Hubinger, Nicholas Carlini, and Jacob Steinhardt.

## Methods

- **SynthesizeMe-inspired:** a coherent persona organized around recurring priorities, research habits, tradeoffs, and representative decisions.
- **ICAI-inspired:** an interpretable constitution of conditional research principles, including scope, exceptions, tensions, and concrete cases.
- **P8:** competing interpretations of the researcher, with counterevidence and unresolved uncertainty.

These are corpus-based adaptations. They do not claim to reproduce preference-data fitting from the original SynthesizeMe or ICAI work.

## Pilot

Generate the two missing Hubinger profiles from existing verified evidence. Do not rerun corpus extraction. Both methods receive the same neutral evidence packet. Each final profile should contain 3,000–4,000 meaningful prose words, preserve exact source identifiers, distinguish direct statements from collaborative findings and inference, and avoid invented biography or claims of validated predictive fidelity.

Record the account credit balance immediately before and after the synthesis calls and retain each call's token receipt. Stop after Hubinger so the user can judge cost and quality before authorizing expansion to Carlini and Steinhardt.
